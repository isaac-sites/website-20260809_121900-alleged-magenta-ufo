## Introduction

The reported 17 March 1943 fire at SIAI’s Vergiate complex is one of the more intriguing pieces of the later Magenta UFO narrative, but it does **not presently provide [independent evidence]({{ 'independent-evidence/' | relative_url }}) that a recovered anomalous craft, fragments or secret [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) records were stored there**. The strongest published version of the claim says that a SIAI official named Aldo Moretti became involved in anti-Fascist activity and deliberately set fire to a company building. UFO researcher Alfredo Lissoni subsequently proposed that the damaged hangar might have contained material connected with the alleged 1933 recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

The crucial distinction is between evidence for a **wartime fire or sabotage incident** and evidence for **what the affected building contained**. The first proposition has at least some historical context: SIAI was a major military-aircraft manufacturer, its workforce experienced wartime unrest, and the wider industrial area became entangled in resistance activity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/varese/cronaca/gli-scioperi-in-fabbrica-a-varese-nel-1943-storia-e-testimonianze-4820200e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gli scioperi in fabbrica a varese nel 1943 storia e testimonianze 4820200e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoGli scioperi in fabbrica a Varese nel 1943: storia e testimonianzeOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span> The second proposition remains unsupported by the sources located: no contemporary inventory, fire report, photograph, police record or company document produced in published accounts identifies UFO wreckage or RS/33 material inside the building.

## What Is Claimed About the March 1943 Fire?

The specific date most often given in the UFO literature is **17 March 1943**. Lissoni's account, reproduced by the Italian Centro Ufologico Nazionale and other UFO archives, says that a SIAI building at Vergiate was deliberately burned. It connects the incident with an employee or official named **Aldo Moretti**, whose name Lissoni says he found in a 6 September 1940 issue of *Zic*, a SIAI employee publication, where Moretti was described as an official of the "D.O." — interpreted, tentatively, as an operational directorate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/hangar.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico NazionaleUNOPENED FILENovember 1, 2000...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 1, 2000</span></span></span>

A later account in *Il Giorno* gives a somewhat fuller chronology. It reports the 17 March fire, followed several days later by an attempt to destroy aircraft ready for departure and, on 3 May, the destruction of an electrical installation belonging to SIAI at Borgomanero. It also says Republican-era security records later referred to people who had gone underground, including "Moretti and Tiferi from [Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }})".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoQuell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascistiNovember 15, 2015...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2015</span></span></span>

That surrounding history is important because political and industrial unrest at SIAI was real rather than something invented for the UFO story. An exhibition organised by the **State Archives of Varese**, drawing upon prefecture and police records, documented strikes and other resistance-related actions at major factories across the province during 1943, specifically including SIAI at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/varese/cronaca/gli-scioperi-in-fabbrica-a-varese-nel-1943-storia-e-testimonianze-4820200e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gli scioperi in fabbrica a varese nel 1943 storia e testimonianze 4820200e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoGli scioperi in fabbrica a Varese nel 1943: storia e testimonianzeOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span> A separate account of the exhibition likewise identifies SIAI workers among those involved in wartime protests and notes arrests and repression connected with industrial action.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.malpensa24.it/scioperi-1943-mostra-varese/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: malpensa24.it">[MALPENSA24]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">malpensa24.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">scioperi 1943 mostra varese</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli scioperi del 1943 in mostra 80 anni dopo all’Archivio di Stato di Varese MALPENSA24...</span></span></span>

This makes an act of sabotage at an important aircraft manufacturer entirely plausible in its ordinary wartime setting. It does not require an extraordinary explanation.

There is also a geographical complication that is easy to lose in later retellings. Some versions describe the incident as a fire in a **Vergiate hangar**; another reconstruction places the arson damage at the SIAI installations at **Sant'Anna**, within the neighbouring Sesto Calende industrial network.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alecappelletti.com/2018/11/15/lufo-di-mussolini-e-il-gabinetto-rs33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alecappelletti.com">[alecappelletti]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alecappelletti.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L’UFO di Mussolini e il Gabinetto RS33 – alecappelletti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L’UFO di Mussolini e il Gabinetto RS33 – alecappellettiNovember 15, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 15, 2018</span></span></span> That inconsistency matters because identifying the precise building is essential if the fire is supposed to corroborate a claim about the contents of a particular secret storage site.

## How the Fire Became Part of the UFO Story

The UFO interpretation depends principally on a chain of associations developed retrospectively.

In Lissoni's reconstruction, a mysterious "Moretti" appears in correspondence associated with the alleged Fascist UFO files. Separately, an Aldo Moretti appears among SIAI personnel. Later records are said to mention a Moretti from Sesto Calende going underground. The 1943 fire is then attributed to Moretti, and the possibility is raised that the building he burned contained whatever survived from the supposed 1933 recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

The argument therefore has several steps:

## What the Fire Actually Establishes

The strongest defensible conclusion is narrower: **there is a reported arson incident within the SIAI industrial environment in March 1943, during a period of genuine political unrest and wartime aircraft production**.

That context is unusually important here. SIAI was not an obscure civilian warehouse operator. By 1940 its Sesto Calende operation reportedly employed about 11,000 workers, making the company a substantial component of Italy's aircraft industry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesto_Calende" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sesto Calende</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sesto Calende</span></span></span> Vergiate was actively engaged in advanced military-aircraft development during the war. The prototype S.M.91 heavy fighter, for example, made its first flight at Vergiate on **11 March 1943**, only six days before the date attributed to the fire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoia-Marchetti_S.M.91" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span></span></span>

That timing supplies a useful reality check. A fire, attempted destruction of aircraft or sabotage at such a facility already had obvious conventional military and political significance. Aircraft, engines, tooling, drawings, fuel, components and production capacity were all valuable wartime targets. The existence of secrecy around particular areas or projects would likewise be unsurprising at a factory developing combat aircraft.

The local political environment was turbulent too. Archival work on the province's 1943 strikes identifies SIAI alongside other important Varese-area manufacturers whose employees engaged in industrial resistance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/varese/cronaca/gli-scioperi-in-fabbrica-a-varese-nel-1943-storia-e-testimonianze-4820200e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gli scioperi in fabbrica a varese nel 1943 storia e testimonianze 4820200e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoGli scioperi in fabbrica a Varese nel 1943: storia e testimonianzeOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span> Evidence of a clandestine SIAI employee therefore strengthens the historical case for anti-Fascist activity inside the company far more directly than it strengthens a UFO-storage hypothesis.

In other words, even if every ordinary element of the fire story were confirmed — the date, deliberate ignition, Moretti's involvement and his subsequent clandestine activity — none would by itself establish the contents of the building.

## The Missing Link Is the Building's Contents

For the fire to become meaningful corroboration of the Magenta story, investigators would need evidence that is independent of the UFO narrative and specifically identifies what was inside the affected structure before it burned.

No such evidence appears in the published accounts located for this question. The missing material would ideally include one or more of the following:

* a contemporary SIAI fire or damage report identifying the building and its function;
* fire-brigade, police, prefecture or military records describing what was destroyed;
* an insurance assessment, factory inventory or reconstruction estimate;
* plans establishing which numbered hangar or workshop burned;
* employee testimony recorded independently of the later UFO investigation;
* photographs showing the damaged structure;
* correspondence indicating that unusual recovered material or exceptionally classified records had been stored there before March 1943;
* documentation securely connecting the same Aldo Moretti to both the fire and the alleged earlier "Moretti" episode.

Without something of that kind, the argument risks becoming circular: Vergiate is proposed as the UFO repository partly because of the fire, while the fire becomes suspicious chiefly because Vergiate has already been proposed as the UFO repository.

That is particularly problematic because the original identification of Vergiate itself was inferential. In the account presenting the "hidden UFO at Vergiate" theory, Lissoni explains that he arrived at the SIAI location by combining geographical and circumstantial clues rather than by citing a 1933 document that directly names a Vergiate hangar as the storage destination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span> The 1943 fire therefore cannot independently confirm Vergiate if its UFO significance depends upon accepting the Vergiate hypothesis in the first place.

## Wartime Destruction Does Not Solve the Evidence Gap

A tempting argument is that the fire explains why decisive records or [physical evidence]({{ 'physical-evidence/' | relative_url }}) no longer exist. That is possible in the abstract, but it creates a serious historical problem: **destruction can explain [missing evidence]({{ 'missing-evidence/' | relative_url }}), but missing evidence cannot prove what was destroyed**.

The principal UFO account illustrates the difficulty clearly. It speculates that by 1943 the alleged material at Vergiate might have ranged from an intact vehicle to fragments, photographs, drawings or documents, while simultaneously acknowledging that its nature and quantity cannot be established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span> Those possibilities are mutually important because they imply very different storage requirements and very different consequences from a fire.

Nor did the March incident end significant aeronautical activity at Vergiate. The S.M.91 programme was being flight-tested there in 1943, while the S.M.95 also flew from Vergiate later that year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoia-Marchetti_S.M.91" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span></span></span> The facility therefore remained sufficiently functional for important aircraft development after the alleged fire. That does not tell us exactly which building burned or how badly it was damaged, but it cautions against imagining, without further documentation, that the March event destroyed the entire site or necessarily eliminated a uniquely important repository.

Later wartime destruction creates another source of confusion. SIAI's facilities became military targets as the war progressed, and Vergiate suffered Allied attacks. Aviation histories record aircraft prototypes being destroyed during later bombing of the airfield.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.anua.it/files/Corr_Aviat_9-10_2019_WEB.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: anua.it">[ANUA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">anua.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Corr Aviat 9 10 2019 WEB</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Corr_Aviat_9-10_2019_WEB.pdf...</span></span></span> Consequently, ruins, lost records or destroyed workshops at the end of the war cannot automatically be attributed to the March 1943 fire, much less to an attempt to eliminate evidence of the alleged 1933 event.

## The Moretti Identification Needs Stronger Proof

Moretti is potentially the most interesting human link in the story, but he is also where careful source criticism matters most.

Published UFO accounts combine several pieces of information: a mysterious "Moretti" mentioned in material associated with the alleged Fascist files; an Aldo Moretti employed by SIAI; and a later security reference to "Moretti and Tiferi" from Sesto Calende entering clandestinity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span> Those details warrant investigation, but sharing a surname and geographical setting does not by itself establish that every reference concerns the same individual.

The chronology also needs to be treated carefully. Later narratives interpret Moretti's supposed change from regime-associated employee to resistance figure as connected with secret information he possessed. Yet SIAI operated amid a much wider pattern of wartime labour resistance. The State Archives of Varese material documents strikes and political opposition among factory workers across the province in 1943.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/varese/cronaca/gli-scioperi-in-fabbrica-a-varese-nel-1943-storia-e-testimonianze-4820200e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gli scioperi in fabbrica a varese nel 1943 storia e testimonianze 4820200e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoGli scioperi in fabbrica a Varese nel 1943: storia e testimonianzeOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span> There was therefore a substantial historical pathway to resistance activity that had nothing to do with anomalous technology.

For Moretti to become strong corroborating evidence, researchers would need a documented biographical chain: full identity, employment record, political history, contemporary attribution of the fire, and — most importantly — evidence showing that his motive involved the alleged recovered object or its documentation. Published UFO accounts presently bridge that final gap through interpretation rather than a demonstrated contemporary record.

## What Would Change the Assessment?

The fire could become important evidence if archival material established three facts simultaneously: **which structure burned, who deliberately burned it, and what it contained**.

A contemporary document stating that Moretti sabotaged a specific Vergiate hangar would substantially improve the historical foundation of the story. A corresponding SIAI inventory showing that the same building held highly classified material transferred from an unidentified 1933 recovery would be much more significant. Evidence predating the modern UFO investigation would be especially valuable because it would remove the possibility that the fire acquired its unusual meaning only after the Magenta narrative had developed.

Physical or documentary continuity would be stronger still. For example, records tracing an unusual object from a 1933 recovery location to a named SIAI facility and then recording its loss, relocation or inspection around March 1943 would turn several currently separate claims into a genuine evidential chain.

What would **not** be enough is simply confirming that the fire happened. A wartime aircraft factory experiencing sabotage is historically notable but not anomalous. Even confirmation that Moretti caused it would demonstrate his actions, not his motive or the contents of the building.

## Verdict: Interesting Context, Not Corroboration

The 17 March 1943 fire is worth investigating because it offers something unusually concrete within the Vergiate strand of the Magenta story: a dated event, an industrial setting and a potentially identifiable person. It also sits within a historically documented period of unrest at SIAI and other Varese factories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/varese/cronaca/gli-scioperi-in-fabbrica-a-varese-nel-1943-storia-e-testimonianze-4820200e" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">gli scioperi in fabbrica a varese nel 1943 storia e testimonianze 4820200e</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il GiornoGli scioperi in fabbrica a Varese nel 1943: storia e testimonianzeOctober 15, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 15, 2023</span></span></span>

But the fire currently supports the UFO story only **indirectly and circumstantially**. The surviving published argument moves from a plausible wartime sabotage incident to an undocumented proposition about what was inside the affected building. Even sources sympathetic to the Magenta interpretation concede that they cannot establish whether the site contained an alleged craft, fragments, documents or anything connected with RS/33 at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

The most defensible assessment is therefore that the fire is **compatible with** the later Vergiate narrative but does not **corroborate** its central extraordinary claim. At present, it is better understood as an unresolved episode in the wartime history of an important Italian aircraft manufacturer onto which a later UFO interpretation has been placed. Until contemporary records identify the burned building and its contents, the crucial connection remains a hypothesis rather than evidence.
