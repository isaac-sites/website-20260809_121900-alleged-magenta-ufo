## Introduction

The June 1933 telegram is the most direct surviving document offered in support of the claim that Benito Mussolini personally ordered secrecy over the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) incident. In published versions, the message says that, “by personal order of the Duce”, absolute silence was required over the presumed landing of an [unknown aircraft]({{ 'unknown-aircraft/' | relative_url }}) on Italian territory. It also directs officials and journalists towards a version supplied by the Fascist regime's Stefani news agency and threatens severe punishment for breaches.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

If authentic, that language would be unusually important: it would connect [Mussolini]({{ 'mussolini/' | relative_url }}) himself to the suppression rather than merely showing that lower-level Fascist officials discussed an unexplained aircraft. The problem is [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}). The papers surfaced anonymously more than six decades later, outside a documented state-archive chain. Even material published by Roberto Pinotti acknowledges that the source was anonymous and therefore doubtful. Moreover, a technical examination often cited in support of the files concerned a different, handwritten document dated 1936, not the crucial June 1933 telegram.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yumpu.com/it/document/view/15948023/gli-x-files-del-fascismo-societa-italiana-di-storia-militare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yumpu.com">[Yumpu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yumpu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO - Societa italiana di storia militare...</span></span></span>

## What the June 1933 telegram reportedly says

The document generally described as the central secrecy telegram is dated [13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }}) 1933 and presented as having passed through the Milan telegraph office, with Agenzia Stefani named as sender. Published accounts describe it as carrying extremely high confidentiality and priority markings. The key instruction is unequivocal: “absolute silence” is supposedly required concerning the presumed landing on Italian soil of an unknown aircraft, and the order is attributed personally to Mussolini.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The rest of the message matters because it describes a mechanism for imposing that silence rather than simply requesting discretion. According to [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }})'s English rendering, a version of events already distributed by Stefani was to stand; that version was to be used with personnel and journalists; and violators faced the maximum penalties, potentially including referral to the regime's state-security tribunal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Published Italian reporting describes a small group of related telegrams rather than one isolated instruction. *Il Giorno* reported that Pinotti and Alfredo Lissoni published three messages dated 13 June, marked highly confidential and attributed to a special-affairs directorate. In that account, a 16:00 telegram pointed towards a meteorite explanation, while a message at 17:07 ordered the immediate stopping of information about an aircraft of unknown nature and origin and the destruction or remelting of newspaper printing material carrying the story. A third communication reiterated the instructions and the meteorite version.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span></span></span>

One reproduced telegram makes the censorship language particularly concrete. Its text attributes the instruction to Mussolini personally, demands the immediate halt of information about an aircraft of unknown nature and origin, refers back to a Stefani dispatch supposedly issued at 07:30 that morning, orders the immediate remelting of newspaper type carrying the information, and again threatens offenders with referral to the state-security tribunal.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATelegramma_Mussolini_13_giugno_1933.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

That sequence is why the telegrams occupy such an important place in the Magenta narrative. They do not merely say that something unusual was observed. Taken at face value, they describe information moving through official channels during a single day, followed by an effort to replace it with a controlled public explanation.

There is, however, an important distinction between what the papers say and what has been independently established. *Il Giorno* notes that the supposed 07:30 telegram announcing the unconventional aircraft was not itself recovered by the UFO researchers; its existence is inferred from references in the surviving messages.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span></span></span> That leaves part of the apparent sequence dependent on internal cross-references within the disputed collection.

## Why the wording would directly implicate Mussolini

The phrase attributing the order personally to the Duce is what elevates this document above the other alleged RS/33 papers when assessing Mussolini's role. A memorandum showing that police, military officers or propagandists wanted secrecy would establish only institutional involvement. This telegram purports to identify the origin of the instruction at the very top.

Its accompanying details also fit real features of Fascist information control. Agenzia Stefani genuinely served as an official news organ of the Fascist government. Treccani records that, under Manlio Morgagni, the agency placed itself fully at the regime's service, received government advantages and funding, and was authorised to alter information to meet propaganda requirements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/agenzia-stefani/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Agenzia stefani</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Agenzia stefani - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

The telegram's supposed use of Stefani as the source of an approved public version is therefore historically plausible. Research published by the University of London Press describes Mussolini's [Press Office]({{ 'press-office/' | relative_url }}) and Agenzia Stefani together as the regime's operational machinery for controlling the press and manipulating information. By the late 1920s the dictatorship had already brought the daily press under extensive political control, and that control was strengthened further during the early 1930s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://read.uolpress.co.uk/read/going-to-the-people/section/c4abd37e-e92f-400f-900a-397cb61f7c28" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: read.uolpress.co.uk">[University of London Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">read.uolpress.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uolpress.co.uk.</span></span></span>

The threatened suppression of newspaper reporting is similarly compatible with the period. Regulations in force by 1931 allowed local public-security authorities to seize newspapers or periodicals that breached official restrictions. Academic work on Fascist media describes a system in which Mussolini's government sought not merely to censor hostile material but to shape what newspapers positively told their readers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://read.uolpress.co.uk/read/going-to-the-people/section/c4abd37e-e92f-400f-900a-397cb61f7c28" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: read.uolpress.co.uk">[University of London Press]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">read.uolpress.co.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on uolpress.co.uk.</span></span></span>

This produces an important evidential distinction. The telegram's bureaucratic scenario is credible in the sense that Fascist Italy possessed the institutions and practices described in it. Stefani existed, the regime controlled news, Mussolini intervened closely in press policy, and coercive sanctions were real. None of those facts authenticates this particular telegram. They establish **contextual plausibility**, not provenance.

Indeed, a sophisticated fabrication made decades later could exploit precisely those genuine historical details. Conversely, an authentic order would naturally contain the same details. Period-appropriate institutions and censorship procedures therefore cannot resolve the central question by themselves.

## What is missing from the document's provenance

The largest weakness is the route by which the papers entered the historical record. Pinotti states that the material concerning the so-called Fascist UFO files was sent anonymously on several occasions and was presented publicly by him and Lissoni at the World UFO Symposium in San Marino in 2000. He also says that other caches from the anonymous sender, dubbed “Mr. X”, went to the Italian UFO organisation CUN, the newspaper *Il Resto del Carlino* and another UFO publication. Some recipients were sceptical: Pinotti's own account says material supplied to another magazine was considered fake, while *Il Resto del Carlino* did not publish what it received.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

That history is fundamentally different from discovering a telegram in a catalogued government file. There is no demonstrated chain running from a Fascist office in June 1933, through documented official custody, into a recognised archive and finally to researchers. Instead, the public chain begins in the 1990s with an unidentified sender. Pinotti himself described the Fascist material received in 1996 as being of anonymous and consequently doubtful origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yumpu.com/it/document/view/15948023/gli-x-files-del-fascismo-societa-italiana-di-storia-militare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yumpu.com">[Yumpu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yumpu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO - Societa italiana di storia militare...</span></span></span>

The distinction matters because document authentication has several layers. A sheet of paper can be old without its text being genuine; a genuine period form can later receive fabricated writing; and even an entirely period-correct document does not automatically prove that the person named in it actually issued the instruction. Strong attribution to Mussolini would ideally require mutually reinforcing evidence such as archival registration marks, an identifiable originating office, receiving copies in another official file, contemporary correspondence referring to the order, or a continuous custody history.

The frequently cited technical examination of the Fascist UFO papers does not close this gap. The published report by consultant Antonio Garavaglia tested the characteristics of paper and ink and concluded that the particular sample examined was compatible with its stated date. But the report explicitly identifies that sample as a **handwritten document dated 22 August 1936**. Its conclusion is limited to that sample. It therefore cannot simply be transferred to the separate typed June 1933 secrecy telegram as proof that the latter is authentic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.yumpu.com/it/document/view/15948023/gli-x-files-del-fascismo-societa-italiana-di-storia-militare" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: yumpu.com">[Yumpu]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">yumpu.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GLI X FILES DEL FASCISMO - Societa italiana di storia militare...</span></span></span>

This is one of the most important qualifications in evaluating claims that “the [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) were authenticated”. Evidence that one item within a broader anonymously supplied body of material appears physically consistent with 1936 does not authenticate every other item in the collection, still less establish that Mussolini personally authorised the words attributed to him.

There are other unresolved documentary questions. Pinotti reports that an original thirty-page dossier sent to *Il Resto del Carlino* was lost, while some of the material supplied elsewhere consisted of photocopies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span> Reproductions can preserve useful textual information, but they sharply restrict forensic testing of paper, ink, typing, impressions and other physical characteristics that might help establish when and how a document was produced.

## How much weight can the telegram bear?

As evidence within the Magenta story, the telegram is unusually consequential but unusually vulnerable. Its language does exactly what proponents of Mussolini's personal involvement need: it names the Duce as the source of the secrecy order, describes the disputed object as an aircraft of unknown character, invokes a real regime news agency and outlines practical censorship measures. The related telegrams appear to reinforce one another by referring to the same day's information and suppression effort.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Historical context also makes the proposed suppression mechanism believable. Fascist Italy already possessed extensive press controls, and Stefani really did function as a central instrument for managing official information. The idea that Mussolini's government could suppress an embarrassing or sensitive event and impose an authorised account on newspapers requires no speculative machinery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/agenzia-stefani/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Agenzia stefani</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Agenzia stefani - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

But those points establish capability and internal plausibility rather than authorship. The decisive weakness remains that the telegram has not been tied through a secure archival provenance to Mussolini's government. The known modern history of the papers begins with anonymous deliveries, and the published physical examination sometimes cited as authentication applies to a different document from 1936.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[The Black Vault]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">The Black Vault THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The Black VaultTHE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrow. The June 1933 telegram is the strongest documentary **claim** that Mussolini personally ordered absolute silence over the alleged Magenta incident. If authenticated, its wording would constitute striking evidence of direct intervention from the dictator. On the evidence presently available, however, it cannot securely establish that intervention as historical fact. The telegram tells us precisely what Mussolini is alleged to have ordered; its unresolved provenance is why historians cannot yet say that he actually ordered it.
