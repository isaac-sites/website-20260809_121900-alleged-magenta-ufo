## Introduction

The Garavaglia examination is often described as having “authenticated the Magenta papers”, but that wording gives the test a much broader reach than the surviving evidence supports. Antonio Garavaglia’s 2000 appraisal concerned **one handwritten document dated 22 August 1936**, associated with an alleged aerial sighting near Mestre. It was not a forensic [authentication]({{ 'authentication/' | relative_url }}) of the anonymous documentary collection as a whole, and it did not test the crucial 1933 Magenta crash telegrams.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/fafil.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalefiles fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalefiles fascisti</span></span></span>

Garavaglia did find physical characteristics that he regarded as consistent with an old document, and he concluded that the stated 1936 date was reliable. That is meaningful evidence about the particular sheet he examined. But material compatibility with the 1930s is different from establishing who wrote a document, which government office created it, how it entered an official archive, or whether the events described in its text actually happened. Modern forensic literature also treats precise document dating as a difficult and method-sensitive problem.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0026265X21008080" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forensic analytical approaches to the dating of documents: An overview - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

## What Pinotti said the testing established

Roberto [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }}) commissioned Garavaglia, a technical consultant registered with the Court of Como, to examine part of the anonymously received material. The surviving report, reproduced online and translated in detail in a later examination of the case, records Pinotti's question as essentially whether the handwriting ink could be regarded as authentic and whether the date written on the document could be considered reliable. The document supplied for testing was the handwritten 22 August 1936 paper, not a representative laboratory sample of every purported Fascist UFO file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

That distinction is particularly important for the alleged Magenta crash. The National UFO Centre's own description identifies Garavaglia's subject as the 1936 handwritten paper on Senate of the Kingdom stationery concerning an unusual object seen around Mestre. The document reportedly described a long, torpedo-like object accompanied by two smaller objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/fafil.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[Centro Ufologico Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Ufologico Nazionalefiles fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Ufologico Nazionalefiles fascisti</span></span></span>

The appraisal was therefore addressing a narrower proposition than later phrases such as “the Fascist UFO files were authenticated” can suggest.

Garavaglia began with an important qualification of his own: according to the translated report, there were no analytical data or precise methods capable of providing certain and proven dating results. He therefore used what he called two empirical approaches. First, the questioned document was compared with handwritten and typewritten material attributed to the relevant historical period. Second, newly prepared writing was subjected to accelerated ageing and environmental stress so that its behaviour could be compared with the questioned material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

For the experimental comparison, Garavaglia prepared a modern sample using black India ink and a nib pen. Portions were then exposed for 27 days to different conditions: storage in darkness at room temperature; ultraviolet radiation at 254 nanometres; warm air at 40°C; and a sequence involving freezing, room temperature and heating. Portions of the questioned document underwent corresponding treatments. The samples were subsequently inspected with a magnifying glass and optical microscope.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

Garavaglia also conducted empirical solubility tests using 95 per cent ethanol, chloroform and a 25 per cent sodium hydroxide solution. The modern experimental ink and the ink from the questioned document behaved differently under these tests. He interpreted those differences, together with the accelerated-ageing observations and comparison with period [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}), as evidence against the proposition that the questioned writing was a recently prepared imitation like his modern control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

His conclusion was correspondingly positive. He said, while explicitly limiting the finding to the tests and sample examined, that the sample could with reasonable certainty be regarded as original and authentic, and that the written date — 22 August in the fourteenth year of the Fascist era, corresponding to 22 August 1936 — was reliable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

Pinotti subsequently presented the result more expansively, describing Garavaglia's chemical examination of paper and ink as establishing that the material was genuinely from the Fascist period rather than a modern forgery. ## Why the methodology remains disputed

The full report now provides more information than was available to some early critics, but it also makes clear why the appraisal should not be treated as equivalent to a modern, independently replicated dating programme.

Most importantly, Garavaglia's procedure was **comparative and empirical rather than an absolute chemical dating measurement**. He compared appearance and behaviour, including the response of the questioned ink to environmental stress and solvents, with historical material and with a newly made India-ink control. The experiment demonstrated differences between the questioned material and that particular modern comparison sample. It did not establish that every plausible modern forgery technique would produce the same behaviour as the control.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

That matters because “not behaving like this freshly prepared control” is a narrower finding than “must have been written in 1936”. A hypothetical fabrication need not have been produced with the particular India ink used by Garavaglia, nor need it have been created shortly before examination. More fundamentally, a forger could in principle begin with genuinely old stationery. CISU archivist Massimiliano Grandi noted that period Senate envelopes and similar historical material survived in substantial quantities, making the mere age or appearance of stationery insufficient to establish official [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The wider forensic literature reinforces the need for caution. A review of analytical approaches describes document dating as one of the more challenging and controversial areas of questioned-document examination. Methods can examine ink, paper, toner and other constituents, but they answer different questions: some establish the earliest possible date from material composition, while others attempt to infer elapsed time from ageing processes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0026265X21008080" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forensic analytical approaches to the dating of documents: An overview - ScienceDirect...</span></span></span>

Ink ageing in particular is not a simple clock. Research on solvent-based dating methods has highlighted unresolved questions about reproducibility, validation and interpretation, even with instrumental methods such as gas chromatography and mass spectrometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0379073811000491" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sciencedirect.com">[ScienceDirect]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sciencedirect.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Minimum requirements for application of ink dating methods based on solvent analysis in casework - ScienceDirectJuly 15, 2011...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2011</span></span></span> The ASTM guide for forensic writing-ink comparison likewise explicitly separates ink comparison from determining an ink's manufacturing date or performing an ink-dating examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://store.astm.org/e1422-01.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: store.astm.org">[ASTM International Store]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">store.astm.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ASTM International StoreE1422 Standard Guide for Test Methods for Forensic Writing Ink ComparisonAugust 16, 2017...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 16, 2017</span></span></span>

This does not demonstrate that Garavaglia's conclusion was wrong. It means that the evidential weight depends on exactly what proposition is being tested. His observations can reasonably be discussed as evidence of physical compatibility with age. They are much weaker as a stand-alone means of fixing a precise creation date.

Early criticism of the Magenta material focused heavily on transparency for precisely this reason. Grandi reported that, when only Garavaglia's conclusions were publicly accessible, specialists consulted through the Maero-Cimini firm considered them too generic to assess without knowing the detailed procedures. CISU argued that independent specialists needed access to both the full methodology and the documents themselves before strong authentication claims could be justified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The subsequent circulation of the fuller report answers part of that complaint because the experimental procedures are now considerably clearer. It does not solve the more important problem of **independent replication on the original material using validated modern methods**.

## Age of materials is not authenticity of the record

The central evidential distinction is between three propositions that are sometimes collapsed into one:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the paper and ink have characteristics consistent with old materials;
* the document was actually created in 1936;
* the document is a genuine record produced or received by the Fascist Italian state and accurately reports the events described.

</div>

Even strong evidence for the first proposition would not automatically establish the second, and neither would by itself establish the third.

For historians and archivists, authenticity involves more than physical ageing. Provenance — the documented history of where a record came from and how it was preserved — matters enormously. The Magenta papers arrived anonymously in the 1990s rather than being recovered from an identified government file. Grandi's archival critique stresses that the surviving “original” pieces lacked the formal archival context needed to establish that they belonged to a public body's records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

This creates a scenario that chemical examination alone cannot eliminate: **old materials can be used to create a newer document**. A sheet of genuinely 1930s stationery would remain 1930s paper regardless of when somebody later wrote on it. Establishing that the writing itself had also aged substantially would narrow that possibility, but the precision required to prove that the ink was applied in one particular year is exactly where forensic document dating becomes difficult. Grandi explicitly raised the question of whether testing could distinguish genuinely old writing from a later document assembled using old paper and old ink, while arguing that the published evidence did not demonstrate that such a distinction had been securely made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

There is an additional limitation specific to the Magenta story: **Garavaglia's tested sheet is not the document that establishes the alleged June 1933 crash**. The examination concerned the 1936 Mestre material. Contemporary descriptions of the collection show that the anonymous deliveries contained several different originals and photocopies, while later reporting on the controversy has specifically noted that the 1933 telegrams were not subjected to Garavaglia's forensic test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

Consequently, even accepting Garavaglia's conclusion at face value would not authenticate the 1933 crash papers by association. Physical findings about one document cannot simply be transferred to separate sheets, telegrams or photocopies merely because they arrived within the same evolving body of anonymous material.

## What the test can reasonably add to the Magenta case

The most defensible reading sits between two common extremes. It is too strong to say that Garavaglia scientifically authenticated the Magenta crash or the entire collection of purported Fascist UFO files. It is also too strong to say that the examination has no evidential value.

The test provides a reason to take the physical characteristics of **one 1936 handwritten document** seriously. Garavaglia examined original material destructively, compared it with historical documents and a modern control, exposed samples to environmental stresses and solvents, and found behaviour he interpreted as consistent with an old rather than freshly fabricated document. His report itself restricted its conclusion to the sample and tests actually performed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.metabunk.org/threads/claim-a-ufo-crashed-in-1933-italy-and-the-us-recovered-it.13282/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: metabunk.org">[Metabunk]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">metabunk.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">claim a ufo crashed in 1933 italy and the us recovered it.13282</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Claim: A UFO crashed in 1933 Italy and the US Recovered it &#124; Metabunk...</span></span></span>

That is the useful finding. It makes a crude modern fabrication of that particular sheet less straightforward than it would be if nobody had examined the original at all. But the appraisal does not independently establish the author's identity, an unbroken 1936-to-1996 chain of custody, official Senate or government provenance, the existence of the alleged [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) organisation, or the truth of the events narrated in the document. CISU's archival assessment likewise did not claim to have proved the papers fraudulent; its conclusion was that serious weaknesses remained in the case for treating them as evidential historical records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

That distinction is the key to understanding the Garavaglia tests. **Material authenticity and historical authenticity are separate questions.** A forensic examination can sometimes show that paper, ink and writing are physically compatible with a claimed period, or reveal materials that make a claimed date impossible. The much larger historical claim requires provenance, archival corroboration, independent examination and evidence connecting the document to the institution and event it purports to record.

For the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) crash, those additional steps remain decisive. Garavaglia's appraisal is best treated as limited forensic evidence concerning one intriguing 1936 artefact — not as laboratory confirmation that an unidentified craft crashed near Magenta in 1933.
