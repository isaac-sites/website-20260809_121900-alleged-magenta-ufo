## Introduction

Allied bombing does confirm that Vergiate was regarded as a genuine military-industrial target. It does **not**, on the evidence presently available, show that Allied intelligence knew of a concealed craft connected with the alleged 1933 Magenta incident. In fact, surviving British planning material supplies a much more ordinary explanation: even before Italy entered the Second World War in June 1940, the Royal Air Force listed Vergiate among the aircraft-production centres whose disruption was a bombing priority.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/photographic-reconnaissance-vol-i-1914-april-1941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force0£</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">W aA.ij?Ha?H *^*Y&#x27;a (q-jBJQ q.sxc^) so^]?ssi?MApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

The frequently repeated UFO argument — that the Americans supposedly bombed the SIAI-Marchetti works nine times because they were trying desperately to destroy something hidden there — therefore reverses the evidential burden. Multiple attacks on a factory making and testing military aircraft are exactly what conventional wartime strategy would predict. A historical chronology does report an initial attack on Vergiate's airfield on 17 June 1940 followed by eight further raids aimed at the SIAI-Marchetti works, but the number alone tells us nothing about an extraordinary object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isspe.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/i-bombardamenti-in-italia-e-nell-impero-durante-la-seconda-guerra-mondiale-di-Pasanisi-vol-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isspe.it">[ISSPE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isspe.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on isspe.it.</span></span></span>

## Why Vergiate was an obvious wartime target

The strongest evidence against reading special significance into the bombing is unusually direct. An RAF Air Historical Branch study records British planning for attacks on Italy before the country entered the war. The Air Ministry's priorities included heavy attacks on the principal industrial centres of Turin, Milan, Genoa and Venice, followed by the disruption of **aircraft airframe and component factories at Turin, Milan, Genoa and Vergiate**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/photographic-reconnaissance-vol-i-1914-april-1941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force0£</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">W aA.ij?Ha?H *^*Y&#x27;a (q-jBJQ q.sxc^) so^]?ssi?MApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

That reference is important because it identifies the reason Vergiate interested British planners in explicitly conventional terms: **aircraft production**. No hidden craft is needed to explain why the place appeared on an Allied target list.

By this stage Vergiate really was an important aviation site. Museo Agusta's history of SIAI states that the [Vergiate hangars]({{ '1933-hangars/' | relative_url }}) had been producing aircraft since 1937 and associates the company with wartime types including the S.79, S.81, S.82 and S.84.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> The factory also supported experimental work. The heavily armed SM.91 fighter prototype, for example, was tested at Vergiate during the war; a second example was later destroyed when bombing struck the airfield.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoia-Marchetti_S.M.91" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span></span></span>

This creates a straightforward military logic:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* Vergiate contained an aircraft factory and airfield.
* SIAI was producing and developing military aircraft.
* British planners explicitly categorised Vergiate with other Italian aircraft-production targets.
* Bombing the plant could reduce Axis aircraft production, testing and repair capacity.

</div>

That explanation rests on documented wartime functions rather than an inference from the mere fact that bombs fell there.

The timing is also significant for the wider Magenta story. Museo Agusta dates aircraft production in the Vergiate hangars to 1937, several years after the alleged 1933 recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> Consequently, the very characteristics that made Vergiate such an attractive wartime target — a substantial aircraft-production complex and associated flying field — are later developments and cannot themselves establish that an anomalous object had been stored at the same site since 1933.

## How the bombing became part of the UFO argument

The bombing claim appears prominently in material associated with Italian UFO researcher Roberto Pinotti and the RS/33 story. One published account of his reasoning says that the recovered object was hypothesised to have been hidden at SIAI facilities at Vergiate or [Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }}). It then argues that the Americans bombed the Vergiate plant nine times while supposedly sparing Sesto Calende, despite its strategically important iron bridge over the Ticino. The implication offered is that the attackers may have been pursuing something exceptional at Vergiate while preserving [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

There are two separate problems with using that argument as evidence.

First, the premise that repeated bombing requires an unusual explanation is undermined by the RAF planning record. Vergiate had already been singled out as an aircraft-industry target in 1940.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/photographic-reconnaissance-vol-i-1914-april-1941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force0£</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">W aA.ij?Ha?H *^*Y&#x27;a (q-jBJQ q.sxc^) so^]?ssi?MApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span> A later Italian bombing chronology likewise describes the 17 June 1940 attack on Vergiate's airport and says eight subsequent attacks were intended to strike the SIAI-Marchetti aeronautical works.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isspe.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/i-bombardamenti-in-italia-e-nell-impero-durante-la-seconda-guerra-mondiale-di-Pasanisi-vol-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isspe.it">[ISSPE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isspe.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on isspe.it.</span></span></span> The surviving historical record therefore supplies an explicit conventional target rationale.

Second, the contrast with supposedly spared Sesto Calende does not hold up well. The municipality of Sesto Calende records that the Sant'Anna seaplane base was known to Anglo-American aviation and was subjected to repeated bombing and strafing together with the nearby Ticino bridge. It also notes surviving bomb damage, unexploded ordnance and structures that survived Allied attacks.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/SantAnna/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Sant&#x27;AnnaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span>

The bridge itself was certainly not preserved throughout the war. Sesto Calende's municipal history states that the earlier iron bridge was destroyed by wartime bombing and replaced after the war.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Il-Punt-da-Feer/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Il Punt da FéerJuly 15, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 15, 2025</span></span></span> A surviving mission list for the US 320th Bombardment Group provides an especially useful contemporary operational anchor: on 18 September 1944, 19 B-26 bombers were assigned to the **Sesto Calende rail and road bridge**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://320thbg.org/1944_missions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 320thbg.org">[320th Bomb Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">320thbg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1944 missions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">320th Bomb GroupB-26 320th Bomb Group 1944 Missions...</span></span></span>

That evidence substantially weakens the claimed Vergiate-versus-Sesto pattern. The Allies were not simply hammering one mysterious factory while leaving the neighbouring strategic infrastructure untouched. They attacked aircraft facilities, airfields, transport links and other military assets across the same district.

## Repeated attacks do not imply a hidden target

The phrase “bombed nine times” sounds suggestive when detached from the realities of strategic bombing. In practice, repeated attacks were common when a target remained operational, had not been sufficiently damaged, was difficult to hit accurately, was repaired after previous raids, or continued to serve the enemy.

Vergiate had several reasons to remain militarily relevant. It combined industrial production with an airfield used for testing, while SIAI aircraft remained part of the Axis aviation system. The wartime fate of the SM.91 illustrates this directly: one prototype was taken to Germany after the 1943 armistice, while another was destroyed in a bombing attack at Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savoia-Marchetti_S.M.91" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti S.M.91</span></span></span> By April 1945, specialist aviation material records S.79 aircraft still under repair or construction, including eight in an advanced stage of construction at SIAI's [Vergiate works]({{ 'vergiate-works/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.alieuomini.it/catalogo/dettaglio_catalogo/savoia_marchetti_sm_sparviero%2C8.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alieuomini.it">[Alieuomini]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alieuomini.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia Marchetti SM.79 Sparviero, Aerei militari, Schede...Risultano ancora in carico 14 trimotori efficienti, 12 in riparazio...</span></span></span>

Nor was repeated targeting peculiar to Vergiate. The 320th Bombardment Group's 1944 mission record shows Allied medium bombers repeatedly returning to bridges, railway lines, fuel dumps, ammunition stores and other infrastructure across northern and central Italy. Individual bridges such as those at Calcinato, Voghera and Cassano d'Adda appear more than once because interdiction was an ongoing operational task rather than a single successful strike.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://320thbg.org/1944_missions.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: 320thbg.org">[320th Bomb Group]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">320thbg.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">1944 missions</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">320th Bomb GroupB-26 320th Bomb Group 1944 Missions...</span></span></span>

The useful question is therefore not “Why would anyone bomb Vergiate so many times?” but “Was Vergiate's documented military value sufficient to explain repeated attacks?” The surviving record strongly indicates that it was.

## The 1940 attack is particularly revealing

The earliest wartime targeting makes the hidden-material interpretation especially difficult.

A chronology of Italian bombing records an attack on Vergiate's airport on **17 June 1940**, only a week after Italy declared war on Britain and France, and says further raids followed against SIAI-Marchetti.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://isspe.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/i-bombardamenti-in-italia-e-nell-impero-durante-la-seconda-guerra-mondiale-di-Pasanisi-vol-2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: isspe.it">[ISSPE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">isspe.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on isspe.it.</span></span></span> Separately, the RAF's historical study shows that Vergiate had already entered British planning as an aircraft-production target before Italy became an active belligerent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/photographic-reconnaissance-vol-i-1914-april-1941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force0£</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">W aA.ij?Ha?H *^*Y&#x27;a (q-jBJQ q.sxc^) so^]?ssi?MApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

This matters because it establishes a documented chain of reasoning independent of the UFO narrative:

**known aircraft industry → pre-war target planning → wartime bombing.**

To replace that explanation with **hidden anomalous material → secret Allied knowledge → bombing campaign**, evidence would be needed showing that Allied planners possessed such intelligence and acted upon it. The fact that the factory was bombed cannot provide that evidence by itself, because the conventional explanation is already documented.

The distinction is easy to miss. A bombing record can establish that Allied intelligence knew **Vergiate mattered**. It cannot, without additional documentation, establish that Allied intelligence knew **why UFO researchers later claimed it mattered**.

## Building survival is not evidence of selective bombing

Another version of the argument points to surviving buildings or hangars and asks whether Allied attacks were somehow selective — perhaps intended to destroy ordinary production facilities while preserving whatever was hidden, or conversely to strike a particular structure containing the alleged object.

Survival alone cannot support such a conclusion. Wartime bombing was not a precise building-by-building instrument in the modern sense. Structures could survive because bombs missed, because damage was repairable, because different raids had different aiming points, or simply because some buildings within a large industrial complex escaped destruction.

The nearby Sant'Anna site demonstrates the danger of reasoning backwards from surviving architecture. Sesto Calende's municipality describes surviving hangars and other structures while simultaneously recording repeated Allied bombing and strafing, unexploded bombs and bomb craters across the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/SantAnna/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Sant&#x27;AnnaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span> A building's survival therefore does not mean that attackers deliberately spared it.

The same standard should apply at Vergiate. A claim of deliberate preservation or unusually concentrated destruction would require operational records, target [photographs]({{ 'photographs/' | relative_url }}), bomb-damage assessments or intelligence files identifying the relevant building and explaining why it mattered. Without that documentary bridge, patterns inferred from what happened to individual structures remain speculative.

## What would actually support the hidden-material theory?

The bombing evidence would become genuinely significant if records connected Allied targeting decisions to intelligence about an unconventional object. The most persuasive material would be something such as an Allied target dossier identifying a particular Vergiate hangar for reasons unrelated to normal aircraft production; intelligence reports describing recovered Italian material of unknown origin; reconnaissance annotations singling out a supposedly secret storage building; or mission orders giving an unusual rationale for destroying or preserving a specific structure.

That is a much higher evidential standard than demonstrating that Vergiate was bombed.

The distinction matters because the underlying UFO claim is itself retrospective. [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }})'s published account describes Vergiate or Sesto Calende as an identification reached through a series of circumstantial clues rather than presenting it as an independently established location in a contemporary Allied bombing document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span> A modern case study sympathetic to renewed investigation likewise describes the transfer of the alleged object to SIAI-Marchetti at Vergiate and its supposed seizure by the US Office of Strategic Services as claims made by Italian researchers, rather than independently documented wartime facts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

That does not make further archival investigation pointless. It clarifies what such research needs to find. The potentially decisive evidence would be **the targeting intelligence behind the raids**, not the raids themselves.

## What the bombing claims really prove

The wartime attacks establish something historically useful but much narrower than the UFO interpretation suggests. They confirm that Vergiate became a significant aviation target and that Allied planners knew its military-industrial importance. British planning explicitly identified Vergiate's aircraft-production capacity as something to disrupt, while wartime records place attacks on the airport and SIAI-Marchetti facilities within that conventional strategic context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/photographic-reconnaissance-vol-i-1914-april-1941/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: raf.mod.uk">[Royal Air Force]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">raf.mod.uk</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Royal Air Force0£</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">W aA.ij?Ha?H *^*Y&#x27;a (q-jBJQ q.sxc^) so^]?ssi?MApril 22, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 22, 2020</span></span></span>

The associated claim that neighbouring Sesto Calende was mysteriously spared is also contradicted by evidence of attacks on Sant'Anna and the Ticino crossing, including a documented US bomber mission against the Sesto Calende rail-and-road bridge in September 1944.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/SantAnna/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Sant&#x27;AnnaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span>

For the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) case, then, the bombing history is **context rather than corroboration**. It explains why Vergiate later looks like a plausible setting for a story about secret aviation material: it really did contain important aircraft facilities, it really was strategically sensitive, and it really was attacked from the air. But none of those facts identifies what, if anything, was secretly stored there.

The strongest available documentary evidence points in the opposite methodological direction: Vergiate did not need a hidden craft to become an Allied target. Its ordinary — and substantial — contribution to Axis aviation was reason enough.
