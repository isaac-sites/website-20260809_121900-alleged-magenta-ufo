## Introduction

The **11 June 1933 theory** is best understood not as a separately documented date for the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) incident, but as an attempted reconciliation of a chronological problem. In this reconstruction, the supposed crash or forced landing happened on **11 June**, while the better-attested **[13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }})** date marks the Fascist authorities’ reaction: censorship orders, telegrams and suppression of press reporting. The two-day separation therefore turns apparently conflicting dates into successive stages of one event.

That solution is neat, but the surviving source trail does not give it the same standing as 13 June. ## How the 11 June reconstruction works

The theory begins with a perfectly reasonable observation about documentary chronology: **the date of an official communication need not be the date of the event that prompted it**. A censorship telegram issued on 13 June could, in principle, concern something that happened on 11 or 12 June.

That distinction matters because the alleged Magenta [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) dated 13 June are framed as administrative reactions. Modern descriptions of the first batch say it contained Senate stationery and confidential telegrams from Milan, all dated **13 June 1933**. One purported order demands silence concerning the presumed landing of an aircraft of unknown nature or origin; another reportedly threatens immediate arrest for spreading information about it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span> Read literally, such wording can describe an event already known to officials rather than something necessarily occurring at the moment the telegram was sent.

The 11 June reconstruction inserts a short interval before those communications:

**11 June:** the alleged aircraft crashes or lands [near Magenta]({{ 'near-magenta/' | relative_url }}).

**11–12 June:** recovery, local reporting or word of the incident supposedly reaches officials.

**13 June:** the government issues censorship instructions and attempts to control the public version.

As a narrative mechanism, this is more coherent than simply assuming that every document bearing 13 June records an event occurring on that same day. The difficulty is that **logical possibility is not evidence for the particular date of 11 June**. Nothing in the publicly described 13 [June telegrams]({{ 'june-telegrams/' | relative_url }}) establishes a two-day interval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

That distinction becomes especially important because some later accounts state the reconciliation as though it were an established correction. One detailed modern retelling says that references to 13 June concern the beginning of public reporting and that the crash “appears” to have occurred two days earlier. The same account consequently places the crash on 11 June in its timeline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/10/mussolinis-flying-saucer-1933.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mussolinis flying saucer 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mussolini&#x27;s flying saucer 1933October 28, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 28, 2023</span></span></span> This is the clearest form of the theory, but it is also evidence of how the theory operates: an interpretation of the dated papers becomes a new event date in the reconstructed chronology.

## Why it separates incident and censorship dates

The appeal of 11 June is that it solves a real interpretative problem without discarding the 13 June documents. If researchers want to retain those papers as evidence while also arguing that authorities had time to recover an object, investigate it and prepare a coordinated press response, an earlier incident date creates useful chronological space.

The wording attributed to the telegrams encourages that reading. According to published transcriptions, the authorities were supposedly ordering “absolute silence” over an **alleged landing** and referring to information circulated through the Stefani news agency. Another communication reportedly refers to a Stefani dispatch dated that morning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/10/mussolinis-flying-saucer-1933.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mussolinis flying saucer 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mussolini&#x27;s flying saucer 1933October 28, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 28, 2023</span></span></span> If genuine, such messages would naturally belong to a response phase rather than serving as an eyewitness record of a crash.

But the documents do not require a two-day gap. An incident early on 13 June could theoretically have produced communications later that day. An event on 12 June could do the same. So could an earlier occurrence. The papers as publicly described establish, at most, that their writers were referring to something treated as already reportable when the messages were drafted. They do not independently select **11 June** from those possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

There is also an important warning against confusing two different “11 June” dates in the modern history of the case. David Grusch's televised NewsNation interview discussing the alleged 1933 Italian recovery was broadcast on **11 June 2023**. Recent case summaries explicitly give that broadcast date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span> That modern date does not establish 11 June 1933, although search results and later retellings can easily place the two dates close together.

## The reconstruction does not solve the April problem

Even if the 11-to-13 June sequence were accepted, it would reconcile only one part of the disputed chronology. A more serious complication is the separate **11 April 1933** date associated with earlier presentations of the Magenta story.

The New Paradigm Institute's review identifies 13 June as the commonly cited incident date and **11 April** as the less frequent alternative. Its inventory of the alleged documents does not identify an 11 June contemporary document; instead, it says the first batch received in 1996 consisted of five items all dated 13 June.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

More strikingly, a later compilation reproducing material attributed to Alfredo Lissoni describes the original claim as a flying saucer that crashed or landed near Magenta on **11 April 1933**, while also saying that three telegrams concerned an object said to have landed on **13 June**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/10/mussolinis-flying-saucer-1933.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mussolinis flying saucer 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mussolini&#x27;s flying saucer 1933October 28, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 28, 2023</span></span></span> Whatever one thinks of those claims, that discrepancy cannot be repaired merely by moving the June incident back two days.

The 11 June theory therefore works as a **local harmonisation**:

**13 June document date → 11 June incident + 13 June censorship**

but it does not provide a demonstrated bridge between:

**11 April incident tradition → June documentary tradition.**

This is one reason the theory should not be treated as though researchers had discovered a missing date in an archive. It is better described as a chronological reconstruction designed to make part of the later narrative internally consistent.

## Why the theory remains weakly sourced

The weakness of 11 June is ultimately inherited from the larger [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) problem surrounding the alleged Fascist UFO papers. The papers publicised by Roberto Pinotti and Alfredo Lissoni arrived anonymously beginning in the 1990s rather than emerging through an identifiable government archive. An archival assessment published by the Italian Centre for UFO Studies, CISU, stresses that their unknown origin and limited availability for independent examination prevent them from carrying the evidential weight of properly provenanced archival records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

That matters even more when trying to infer a date that apparently **is not written on the core 1933 papers themselves**. The New Paradigm Institute's document inventory lists the first batch as entirely dated 13 June. It also notes claims that the paper and ink underwent period testing, but says complete laboratory reports have not been publicly released for independent verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span> CISU's archival analysis similarly argues that tests capable of indicating that paper is old would not establish that the extraordinary claims written upon it are authentic government history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

There is a further problem with retrospective corroboration. CISU researchers located genuine Fascist-era files concerning unidentified or suspicious aircraft in the Milan State Archives, but their archival assessment says these were ordinary aviation-security records rather than confirmation of RS/33 or an extraterrestrial recovery. The same critique notes that contemporary administrative material can survive openly in archives even though no comparable archival trail has been established for the supposed secret organisation at the centre of the Magenta story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

One modern account explicitly advances the proposition that 13 June was when reporting began and that the crash occurred two days earlier, but its own discussion also reproduces older material giving **13 June** and even **11 April** for the incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com/2023/10/mussolinis-flying-saucer-1933.html?m=1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com">[garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">garagegraphicshistory.blogspot.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">mussolinis flying saucer 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mussolini&#x27;s flying saucer 1933October 28, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 28, 2023</span></span></span> Rather than demonstrating that 11 June has displaced earlier dates through new evidence, this shows how several incompatible chronologies have accumulated within the same literature.

## What 11 June actually explains

The most defensible role for the 11 June theory is therefore narrow. It demonstrates that **13 June need not represent both the alleged incident and the alleged suppression effort**. That is a useful chronological distinction: administrative documents can post-date the events that cause them, and the language attributed to the Magenta telegrams is compatible with a government responding to something that had already happened.

What the theory does **not** demonstrate is that the preceding event occurred specifically on 11 June. The publicly described documentary sequence supplies no securely provenanced 11 June crash record, contemporary newspaper report or archival entry that establishes that date. Major modern case summaries instead continue to identify 13 June and 11 April as the principal dates in circulation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

For the disputed Magenta timeline, the distinction is consequential. **13 June is a date appearing on the alleged core papers; 11 June is principally an inferred incident date used to reinterpret those papers.** Separating those two levels of evidence prevents an attractive reconciliation from being mistaken for an independently corroborated chronology. Until a contemporary, traceable source specifically connects the alleged Magenta incident with 11 June 1933, the two-day sequence remains a modern harmonising reconstruction rather than a resolved date for the alleged crash.
