## Introduction

The shape of the alleged Magenta craft cannot currently be proven. The case is built around later-released [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}), interpretations and claims of recovered material, but no independently verified object, fragment, photograph, engineering record or continuous chain of custody has established what the supposed craft looked like. As a result, descriptions such as “disc-shaped”, “bell-shaped”, or “cylindrical” remain competing [reconstructions]({{ 'reconstructions/' | relative_url }}) rather than confirmed physical observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coloradomufon.org/the-magenta-case-italys-1933-ufo-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coloradomufon.org">[Colorado MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coloradomufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on coloradomufon.org.</span></span></span>

## What evidence exists for the Magenta craft’s shape

The strongest shape-related evidence comes from the alleged RS/33 documents publicised by Italian UFO researcher Roberto [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }}) and others from the 1990s onwards. These materials reportedly included descriptions of an unidentified object, including references to a metallic disc with porthole-like features and sketches associated with unusual aircraft sightings. However, the documents themselves are not a confirmed physical record of the object, and their [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) remains disputed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coloradomufon.org/the-magenta-case-italys-1933-ufo-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coloradomufon.org">[Colorado MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coloradomufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on coloradomufon.org.</span></span></span>

### The disc description

The disc-shaped version is the most widely repeated image of the Magenta craft. Accounts connected with the alleged [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) papers describe a metallic object resembling a flying disc, sometimes with structural details such as openings or portholes. This description has had a strong influence because it matches the familiar “flying saucer” form that became dominant in popular UFO culture after the late 1940s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coloradomufon.org/the-magenta-case-italys-1933-ufo-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coloradomufon.org">[Colorado MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coloradomufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on coloradomufon.org.</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that the disc description does not come from a verified examination of a recovered vehicle. Instead, it comes through a chain of documents and later publications. The shape therefore provides evidence about how the Magenta story was recorded and interpreted, but not direct proof of the object’s physical design.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">case study magenta italy 1933</span></span></span>

### Other shapes in later accounts

Other retellings have presented the alleged craft differently, including bell-like or cylindrical forms. Some accounts associated with Magenta describe an unusual aircraft with a more elongated or non-disc appearance, while later discussions have connected the case with a bell-shaped craft concept.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">case study magenta italy 1933</span></span></span>

These differences matter because a physical object recovered in 1933 should normally produce a relatively stable description across official reports, technical notes and surviving records. Instead, the available material shows variation, suggesting that later interpretation has played a role in shaping the image of the craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on factually.co.</span></span></span>

## Missing physical confirmation keeps the shape unresolved

The biggest limitation for determining the Magenta craft’s shape is the absence of publicly verified material evidence. Claims connected with the case state that the object was recovered, studied and eventually moved from Italy, but no authenticated remains have been produced for independent examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">case study magenta italy 1933</span></span></span>

A confirmed artefact would change the debate by allowing researchers to test basic questions: what materials were used, what manufacturing methods were involved, whether the object matched known aircraft technology of the period, and whether historical descriptions corresponded to the physical structure. Without such evidence, shape claims remain dependent on documents and narratives rather than measurable features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">case study magenta italy 1933</span></span></span>

The same issue applies to alleged sketches and document descriptions. A drawing can show what someone reported or imagined, but it cannot by itself establish that the depicted object existed. For the Magenta case, the missing link is not simply a clearer picture of the craft; it is a verified connection between the claimed event, the described object and a surviving physical source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.coloradomufon.org/the-magenta-case-italys-1933-ufo-mystery/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: coloradomufon.org">[Colorado MUFON]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">coloradomufon.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on coloradomufon.org.</span></span></span>

## How uncertainty shapes the debate

The unresolved shape of the Magenta craft affects how the entire case is assessed. Supporters of the claim often point to the unusual nature of the reported descriptions and the alleged RS/33 material as evidence that something significant was investigated. Critics argue that the lack of independently verified documents and physical remains prevents the shape claims from being treated as historical fact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on factually.co.</span></span></span>

The shape question therefore becomes a test of evidence quality rather than a simple choice between different craft designs. A disc, bell or cylinder may each appear in parts of the [Magenta narrative]({{ 'narrative-expansion/' | relative_url }}), but none has been confirmed through a documented chain from observation to object. Until that gap is filled, the craft’s appearance remains an unresolved element of the alleged crash rather than an established feature of a historical event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on factually.co.</span></span></span>
