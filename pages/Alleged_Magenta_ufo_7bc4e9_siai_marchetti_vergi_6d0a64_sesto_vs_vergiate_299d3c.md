## Introduction

If the question is which SIAI location best fits **June 1933**, the stronger historical answer is **[Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }}), including the nearby Sant’Anna aviation complex, rather than the later purpose-built works at Vergiate**. A particularly useful contemporary check comes from the 1933 *Enciclopedia Italiana*: its survey of Italy’s aircraft industry identifies the Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia (SIAI) as having its workshops at **Sesto Calende**. It does not identify Vergiate as the company’s manufacturing site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

That distinction creates a significant problem for the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) story when it is told as a recovered object being taken directly to the “SIAI-Marchetti hangars at Vergiate” in 1933. Institutional histories date SIAI’s new Vergiate factory and associated flying field to **1937**, while the major surviving factory building has been dated to 1938.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[culturadimpresa.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> The evidence therefore supports a narrower conclusion: SIAI was certainly operating in the area in 1933, but the location securely associated with those operations at that date was Sesto Calende/Sant’Anna. Vergiate fits the company’s later geography much better than its documented 1933 geography.

## SIAI's real operating geography in 1933

SIAI was not merely nominally connected with Sesto Calende. It had been rooted there since its foundation in 1915. The historical archive of Fondazione Fiera Milano lists the company’s seat as Sesto Calende, while the Centro per la cultura d’impresa says production began in workshops there. Museo Agusta likewise places the company’s foundation at Sesto Calende and its seaplane base at nearby Sant’Anna on Lake Maggiore.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archiviostorico.fondazionefiera.it/entita/1288-siai-marchetti" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archiviostorico.fondazionefiera.it">[fondazionefiera.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archiviostorico.fondazionefiera.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Fondazione Fiera Archives SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fondazione Fiera ArchivesSIAI Marchetti - Archivio Fondazione Fiera Milano...</span></span></span>

Sant’Anna is important because “Sesto Calende” did not mean a single office building. It formed part of a working aviation landscape. The municipality of Sesto Calende records that the Sant’Anna seaplane base was used for launching and recovering aircraft made by SIAI, with company facilities nearby. A local history of the seaplane base says that SIAI initially used industrial buildings at Sant’Anna for aircraft production.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Parco EuropaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span>

Most importantly for the disputed date, this was not merely SIAI’s early history from the First World War. Sesto Calende remained visibly active at exactly the period in question. The municipality states that the S.55X flying boats used in Italo Balbo’s celebrated 1933 transatlantic formation flight were built at **Sesto Calende**. The Province of Varese similarly describes Sant’Anna as an important launching site for the S.55 during the 1920s and 1930s, including the 1933 Atlantic flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Parco EuropaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span>

There is also unusually strong period evidence. The *Enciclopedia Italiana* article on Italy, published in the 1933 edition, lists the principal aircraft manufacturers and their works. Alongside Caproni at Taliedo, Macchi at Varese and Breda at Sesto San Giovanni, it places **SIAI’s workshops at Sesto Calende**. Because this description comes from the period under discussion rather than a retrospective account, it is especially useful for testing claims about where a SIAI facility was understood to be in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

The company name itself also warns against projecting later terminology backwards. Treccani records that the firm founded as Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia changed its name in 1930, while the corporate name incorporating “Savoia Marchetti” dates from 1937 and the later SIAI-Marchetti form from 1944. Alessandro Marchetti had already been technical director since 1922 and his surname was attached to the aircraft, so historians understandably use “Savoia-Marchetti” or “SIAI-Marchetti” as convenient shorthand. But a description such as “the SIAI-Marchetti factory at Vergiate in 1933” combines a later corporate label with a site whose major development also belongs to a later phase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

## Why Vergiate creates a chronological problem

The clearest industrial history found for the company divides its development into periods and states that in **1937** SIAI built a **new factory at Vergiate**, a few kilometres from Sesto Calende, with an adjoining flying field. That wording matters: Vergiate is presented as an expansion of the established Sesto Calende operation, not as the principal SIAI factory already functioning there in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span>

Other independent sources broadly reinforce that chronology. Museo Agusta says that the [Vergiate hangars]({{ '1933-hangars/' | relative_url }}) had hosted aircraft production since 1937. Material from the Polytechnic University of Milan describes Vergiate as an important aviation site “since 1937”, when SIAI began fixed-wing production there. FlightGlobal, discussing the enormous industrial building still used for helicopter production decades later, dates that structure specifically to **1938**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

This does not justify the stronger claim that absolutely nothing aeronautical could have existed at Vergiate before 1937. The history is slightly messier than that. General histories of Vergiate airport place its creation in the **mid-1930s**, and some secondary aircraft histories associate early Savoia-Marchetti test flying with Vergiate before the new factory's conventionally cited 1937 opening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeroporto_di_Vergiate" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aeroporto di Vergiate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aeroporto di Vergiate</span></span></span> These references make it prudent not to argue from absence: an early field, preliminary activity or infrastructure development around Vergiate cannot simply be ruled out.

But they do not solve the specific **June 1933** problem. The institutional histories consistently place the purpose-built Vergiate manufacturing complex later, while a source actually published in 1933 identifies SIAI’s workshops at Sesto Calende. The defensible distinction is therefore not “Sesto Calende existed and Vergiate did not”; it is that **Sesto Calende is directly documented as SIAI's operating industrial location in 1933, whereas the familiar [Vergiate works]({{ 'vergiate-works/' | relative_url }}) belong to the subsequent expansion of the company**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[treccani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

That distinction is especially important when evaluating [photographs]({{ 'photographs/' | relative_url }}) or descriptions of large Vergiate hangars. The impressive factory architecture associated with the site today is precisely the kind of infrastructure that makes Vergiate sound plausible as a secret storage location. Yet FlightGlobal dates the major building to 1938. Using that later physical setting to visualise events supposedly occurring in June 1933 silently moves infrastructure backwards by roughly five years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightglobal.com/analysis/analysis-new-discipline-new-drive-at-leonardo-helicopters/127027.article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightglobal.com">[FlightGlobal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightglobal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on flightglobal.com.</span></span></span>

## How Sesto Calende and Vergiate became conflated

The conflation is understandable because the two locations eventually formed parts of the same company's tightly connected aviation landscape. Vergiate lies only a few kilometres from Sesto Calende, and after the later factory and airfield were developed there, SIAI's history genuinely belonged to both places. Modern histories therefore discuss “Sesto Calende and Vergiate” together when describing the company across its entire lifespan. That is harmless for a century-long corporate history but risky when testing a claim tied to one particular day in 1933.

The UFO literature itself illustrates how this geographical compression developed. In an account published by the Italian UFO organisation CUN, researcher Roberto Pinotti describes the identification of **“Vergiate or Sesto Calende”** as a hypothesis reached from circumstantial clues: the supposed recovery was thought to have occurred in Lombardy, SIAI had aviation facilities in the area, and various personalities associated with Italian aviation had connections there. The passage is valuable because it makes clear that the SIAI storage location was an **inference**, rather than a location explicitly established by the alleged original recovery papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

That same account then assigns different functions to the neighbouring places, describing Sesto Calende as containing management offices and Vergiate as containing the “real” factories.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span> Read as a description of SIAI's mature, later industrial geography, that distinction is recognisable. Applied unchanged to June 1933, however, it conflicts with the contemporary *Enciclopedia Italiana*, which explicitly locates SIAI's workshops at Sesto Calende, and with the company history that dates construction of the new Vergiate plant to 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

Later retellings tend to eliminate this uncertainty. For example, *La Stampa* reported the claim that the supposed wreckage was kept in SIAI-Marchetti hangars at Vergiate for twelve years.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lastampa.it/cronaca/2017/03/20/news/il-mistero-dell-ufo-che-spavento-mussolini-e-spari-in-america-1.34638554/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lastampa.it">[La Stampa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lastampa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">La Stampa Il mistero dell&#x27;Ufo che spaventò Mussolini e sparì in America</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">La Stampa Il mistero dell&#x27;Ufo che spaventò Mussolini e sparì in America</span></span></span> A 2024 article in the regional newspaper *La Prealpina*, by contrast, described the story as involving an object supposedly taken **first to Sesto Calende and then to Vergiate**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.prealpina.it/news/valli-e-laghi/326391/lufo-nascosto-a-sesto-e-vergiate.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: prealpina.it">[Prealpina]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">prealpina.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">lufo nascosto a sesto e vergiate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">lufo nascosto a sesto e vergiate</span></span></span> Those are materially different versions of the storage chronology.

The variation is revealing. “Vergiate” can appear in popular retellings as though it were an established fact of the alleged 1933 incident, yet the underlying historical argument has circulated in forms ranging from **Vergiate**, to **Vergiate or Sesto Calende**, to **Sesto Calende followed by Vergiate**. That instability is exactly what would be expected if a later-famous aviation location had gradually become attached to an older and poorly documented claim.

## Which location better matches the alleged timeline?

For evaluating the alleged Magenta recovery story, the two locations should therefore be ranked differently rather than treated as interchangeable.

**Sesto Calende/Sant’Anna has the stronger 1933 fit.** SIAI was founded there; production facilities were established there; Sant’Anna provided an associated seaplane base; contemporary evidence identifies SIAI workshops at Sesto Calende; and aircraft for one of the most famous aviation events of 1933 were being built there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[culturadimpresa.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> If an argument merely requires a substantial SIAI aviation establishment capable of handling unusual aeronautical material in 1933, Sesto Calende is the location for which the historical record provides the clearest foundation.

**Vergiate has the stronger later-industrial fit.** Its purpose-built factory, airfield, large hangars and eventual importance in aircraft production make it an intuitively attractive setting for the story. But the best institutional histories place that development in the second half of the 1930s: the new factory in 1937, with the major surviving building dated to 1938.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[culturadimpresa.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> Those later characteristics cannot establish what facilities were available there four years earlier.

There is consequently a plausible mechanism for how the traditional wording arose without requiring deliberate invention. Someone reasoning retrospectively that secret aeronautical material would have gone to “the SIAI works near Lake Maggiore” could encounter a later industrial history dominated by Vergiate and attach that better-known site to the 1933 episode. Because Sesto Calende and Vergiate are neighbouring places associated with the same company, the substitution sounds geographically harmless. Chronologically, it is not.

None of this establishes that an anomalous craft was stored at Sesto Calende. The available historical evidence establishes **SIAI's location**, not the alleged UFO's location. Pinotti's own published explanation treats the identification of the storage area as an inference from surrounding clues rather than a direct statement in the purported 1933 documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span> Moving the proposed storage site from Vergiate to Sesto Calende therefore repairs one chronological weakness in the story, but it does not supply independent evidence that the claimed storage happened.

## The most defensible reading

The strongest reconstruction is narrower than either the sceptical or sensational versions of the story. In **1933**, SIAI unquestionably had a substantial aviation presence around **Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna**. A contemporary reference work identifies its workshops there, and local and institutional histories confirm active aircraft production and seaplane operations at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[treccani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

Vergiate became an equally real and eventually very important part of SIAI's industrial geography, but the dedicated works conventionally associated with the name belong principally to the company's expansion from the **mid-to-late 1930s**, with authoritative institutional sources placing the new plant in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> Some secondary aviation histories point to flying activity around Vergiate somewhat earlier, so the evidence does not support claiming that the locality had no aviation relevance whatsoever before 1937. What it does support is rejecting the casual assumption that the famous later Vergiate factory can be projected intact into June 1933.

For the alleged Magenta UFO case, that leaves a useful but limited conclusion. **If the storage hypothesis is required to fit SIAI's documented geography as it actually stood in 1933, Sesto Calende/Sant’Anna is the better candidate.** Vergiate appears more likely to be a later geographical conflation, or at minimum a location whose familiar industrial significance has been backdated. The correction strengthens the historical accuracy of the setting; it does not authenticate the alleged recovery itself.
