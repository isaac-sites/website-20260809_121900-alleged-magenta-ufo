## Introduction

Sesto Calende creates a real historical problem for one of the most repeated details in the alleged 1933 Magenta UFO story: that a recovered object was taken directly to SIAI aircraft hangars at Vergiate. The difficulty is not that Vergiate lacked an aviation connection. It later became one of SIAI-Marchetti's major production centres. The difficulty is **when** that happened.

For 1933 itself, the documentary trail points strongly to neighbouring Sesto Calende. SIAI had been founded there in 1915; a contemporary 1933 Italian encyclopaedia identified its workshops at Sesto Calende; an official government publication that year described Alessandro Marchetti as technical director of the company "in Sesto Calende"; and SIAI's S.55X flying boats for Italo Balbo's celebrated 1933 Atlantic flight were built there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[treccani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

Vergiate's own municipal historical documentation, by contrast, says Savoia Marchetti acquired the former Polverificio Rossi property in **1935** and began constructing an airfield and new factories there **from 1937**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://storage.googleapis.com/stu-vergiate-public-prod/2022-04/RA%202%20Quadro%20conoscitivo%20I%20Parte.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: storage.googleapis.com">[Google Cloud Storage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">storage.googleapis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Google Cloud Storage VAS – Rapporto Ambientale Comune di Vergiate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Cloud StorageVAS – Rapporto Ambientale Comune di VergiateJune 7, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2014</span></span></span> This does not prove that no SIAI-associated property of any kind existed at Vergiate before 1935. It does mean that the familiar claim of storage in "the SIAI hangars at Vergiate" requires a missing piece of evidence if it is supposed to describe events in 1933.

## Sesto Calende was SIAI's established home

The distinction begins with the company's origins. Treccani records that SIAI was founded at **Sesto Calende in 1915** as the Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia. The corporate name changed in 1930 to Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia Savoia, while Alessandro Marchetti's name was formally incorporated into the company title only in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

That naming history matters because modern accounts often use "SIAI-Marchetti" or "Savoia-Marchetti" retrospectively. Such shorthand is understandable, but it can obscure a more important geographical change: the company associated so strongly with Vergiate in later decades was already an established industrial concern at Sesto Calende long before the alleged Magenta incident.

Museo Agusta's company history confirms the same sequence. It places SIAI's foundation at Sesto Calende by Domenico Santoni and Luigi Capé, with its seaplane base established at Sant'Anna on Lake Maggiore. Alessandro Marchetti became technical director in 1922, and under his direction the company produced aircraft including the S.51 and the internationally famous S.55.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

The waterfront location was fundamental to SIAI's early business. This was an aircraft manufacturer specialising initially in seaplanes, so proximity to water was part of its production and testing infrastructure. Sesto Calende's municipal history describes today's Parco Europa at Sant'Anna as occupying the former military seaplane base, which was used to launch and recover seaplanes manufactured by the nearby SIAI works.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[comune.sesto-calende.va.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comune di Sesto Calende</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parco Europa...</span></span></span>

In other words, Sesto Calende was not merely the place where SIAI happened to have been incorporated. It was a functioning aviation-industrial centre.

## The evidence becomes unusually strong in 1933 itself

For the Magenta controversy, the most useful evidence is not a general company history but documentation tied directly to **1933**.

Treccani's material from the *Enciclopedia Italiana* is particularly significant. Its 1933 discussion of Italian industry lists the "Società idrovolanti Alta Italia (SIAI)" with its workshops at **Sesto Calende**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/tag/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">siai marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">siai marchetti - Tag e risultati - Treccani...</span></span></span> That is contemporary evidence rather than a later historian reconstructing the company's geography.

An official Italian government source provides another contemporary anchor. A September 1933 issue of the *Gazzetta Ufficiale del Regno d'Italia* identifies engineer Alessandro Marchetti as technical director of the Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia **in Sesto Calende**. The same page also identifies an aircraft designer and constructor at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1933/09/30/228/sg/pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gazzettaufficiale.it">[Gazzetta Ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gazzettaufficiale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gazzetta Ufficiale GAZZETTA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF I PARTE PRIMA DEL REGNO D&#x27;ITALIA Si PU...</span></span></span>

There is also a concrete production example from precisely that year. Sesto Calende's municipality records that the S.55X flying boats used in Italo Balbo's famous 1933 transatlantic formation flight were built at Sesto Calende. The municipal description of the former Sant'Anna seaplane base explicitly connects the surviving landscape to those aircraft and to SIAI's nearby factory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[comune.sesto-calende.va.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Comune di Sesto Calende</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Parco Europa...</span></span></span>

These sources reinforce one another in useful ways. Treccani identifies SIAI's workshops at Sesto Calende; the official gazette associates the company's technical director with Sesto Calende; municipal industrial heritage records place actual SIAI aircraft production there in 1933; and Museo Agusta independently traces the company's origins and seaplane operations to the same town.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/tag/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[treccani.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">siai marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">siai marchetti - Tag e risultati - Treccani...</span></span></span>

That combination makes Sesto Calende the securely documented SIAI location against which claims about Vergiate in 1933 have to be tested.

This matters because historical claims can acquire false precision through retrospective naming. It would go too far to conclude from the 1935 and 1937 dates that SIAI could not possibly have possessed **anything** at Vergiate in 1933.

That distinction matters. Historical absence should not be converted into a stronger negative claim than the sources support.

Yet the burden of evidence still falls on the Vergiate claim. If an earlier SIAI warehouse or hangar existed there and was suitable for concealing a recovered aircraft-sized object, it should be demonstrated with evidence dating from 1933 or earlier. The searches underlying this report found strong contemporary evidence for SIAI at Sesto Calende, but no comparably strong 1933 source establishing the Vergiate hangars invoked in the UFO narrative.

That asymmetry is the important finding.

## Sesto Calende should not become an alternative UFO site

There is a temptation to respond to the Vergiate problem by simply moving the alleged object to Sesto Calende. That would not be justified.

Sesto Calende certainly offers a more firmly documented SIAI industrial setting for 1933. It had manufacturing activity, technical personnel, a seaplane base and facilities associated with one of Italy's best-known aircraft companies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

But none of those facts constitutes evidence that extraterrestrial or otherwise unidentified wreckage was ever stored there.

Indeed, Pinotti's reproduced account demonstrates why caution is necessary: it presents Vergiate as the storage location in one passage while describing Lissoni's underlying hypothesis more broadly as involving SIAI facilities at "Vergiate or Sesto Calende".<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span> That is evidence of uncertainty within the reconstruction, not evidence that Sesto Calende was the true secret repository.

Sesto Calende's proper role in assessing the case is therefore as a **historical control**. It shows where SIAI can actually be demonstrated to have been operating at the relevant date.

## What evidence would close the gap?

A convincing resolution of the Vergiate problem would require evidence that bridges the years between the alleged 1933 recovery and the documented mid-1930s development of SIAI's Vergiate complex.

The most useful discovery would be a securely dated company document, cadastral or property record, government industrial register, military correspondence, factory plan, contemporary map, insurance schedule or local administrative record demonstrating that SIAI controlled an appropriate facility at Vergiate in or before 1933.

Such a document would substantially change the assessment. It could show that the 1935 purchase recorded by the municipality represented an expansion of an already existing Vergiate presence rather than SIAI's effective arrival at the site.

Conversely, a detailed property history showing that the relevant land and buildings remained under other ownership until 1935 would make the familiar UFO narrative still harder to sustain in its present form.

Until evidence of that kind appears, later facts should not be used as substitutes. The existence of SIAI factories at Vergiate during the Second World War does not prove their existence in 1933. Nor does the later fame of Vergiate as an aviation centre establish the location of an alleged secret transfer years earlier.

The result is not proof that the alleged Magenta recovery never occurred. It is narrower but important: **the historical record presently supports Sesto Calende much more clearly than Vergiate as SIAI's established industrial location in 1933**.

Until a securely dated pre-1935 Vergiate facility can be demonstrated, Sesto Calende remains the missing geographical and chronological check that prevents the later SIAI-Marchetti landscape from being projected uncritically backwards onto 1933.
