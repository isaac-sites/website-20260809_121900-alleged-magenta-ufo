## Introduction

The alleged [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) telegrams are judged not only by what they claim to describe, but also by whether their appearance and administrative style resemble genuine Fascist-era communications. The short answer is that some features appear compatible with 1930s Italian bureaucratic practice, but the surviving material has not provided enough independently verifiable archival context to establish authenticity. The debate centres on a familiar historical-document problem: a paper can look period-appropriate while still being difficult to connect to a real office, filing system, or chain of command.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

## How Fascist telegrams were structured

Italian state telegrams of the 1930s followed established bureaucratic conventions rather than functioning as informal letters. Official communications commonly identified the sender and recipient, included dates and places of dispatch, used numbered references where part of an administrative exchange, and separated the message content from transmission details. Surviving diplomatic records from Fascist Italy show this broader pattern: telegrams were embedded in government correspondence systems with clear sender–recipient relationships and reception records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1938v02/d472" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the HistorianOctober 17, 1938...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 1938</span></span></span>

A key feature of period telegrams was that the physical message was only one part of a larger administrative process. A convincing telegram would normally fit into a paper trail: who sent it, which office authorised it, who received it, how it was registered, and what later [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) referred back to it. This wider context matters because formatting alone is not the same as [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://history.state.gov/historicaldocuments/frus1938v02/d472" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: history.state.gov">[Office of the Historian]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">history.state.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of the Historian Historical Documents</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of the HistorianHistorical Documents - Office of the HistorianOctober 17, 1938...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 17, 1938</span></span></span>

The alleged RS/33 material is debated partly because it presents fragments of that bureaucratic appearance without the full documentary ecosystem that historians usually rely upon. The papers contain features that resemble official communication styles, but the public record does not provide a continuous archive showing the documents moving through known Fascist institutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.pepijnvanerp.nl/2023/06/mussolinis-ufo-cabinet-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: pepijnvanerp.nl">[Pepijn van Erp]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">pepijnvanerp.nl</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini’s UFO – Cabinet RS/33 – Pepijn van Erp</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Pepijn van Erp Mussolini’s UFO – Cabinet RS/33 – Pepijn van Erp</span></span></span>

## Formatting clues in the alleged papers

The strongest argument in favour of the RS/33 telegrams’ plausibility is that they contain details that do not look like a simple modern invention. Researchers associated with the documents have pointed to elements such as telegram terminology, urgency indicators, and references to Fascist-era institutions. Some defenders argue that these small administrative details would be difficult for a casual fabricator to reproduce accurately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

One example often discussed is the use of urgent communication terminology. Supporters have argued that expressions associated with rapid military or government handling resemble real administrative practice of the period. However, critics note that a single authentic-sounding term does not prove the surrounding document is authentic. A forged or altered document can incorporate genuine bureaucratic language while attaching it to a fictional event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

The classification style is another disputed area. The alleged papers use secrecy language intended to suggest an unusually sensitive government matter. Critics have questioned whether the level and wording of [secrecy markings]({{ 'secrecy-markings/' | relative_url }}) match what would be expected for an event supposedly involving an unknown craft and high-level state attention. The issue is not simply whether secret markings existed, but whether the particular combination of wording, distribution assumptions, and administrative handling fits the seriousness of the claimed subject.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

There is also a distinction between the appearance of a telegram and the operation of a real telegram network. Historical examples show that government telegrams were tied to established communications channels and institutional procedures.
