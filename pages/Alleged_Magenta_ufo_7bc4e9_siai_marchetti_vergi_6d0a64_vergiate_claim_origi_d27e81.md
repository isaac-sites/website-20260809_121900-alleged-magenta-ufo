## Introduction

Vergiate entered the alleged 1933 Magenta UFO story not because the surviving published papers clearly name it as the place where a recovered craft was stored, but because researchers **reconstructed a likely destination decades later**. The key figure in that reconstruction was Italian UFO researcher Alfredo Lissoni. His published account says that, working from supposed Milan-originating communications, the aviation industry around Lake Maggiore, links to Italo Balbo and other circumstantial clues, he proposed that the object might have been concealed at SIAI facilities in **Vergiate or neighbouring [Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }})**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

That distinction changes how the familiar claim should be read. SIAI genuinely was a major aircraft manufacturer at Sesto Calende in 1933, giving the regional hypothesis a real historical foundation. But independent industrial histories date SIAI's **new Vergiate factory to 1937**, four years after the alleged recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> Vergiate is therefore best understood as the end product of an investigative inference that gradually hardened into a definite location in later retellings, rather than as a storage site directly identified by a verified contemporary record.

## The location was reconstructed after the documents surfaced

The modern [Magenta narrative]({{ 'narrative-expansion/' | relative_url }}) grew from papers received anonymously by Italian UFO researchers in the 1990s. A recent preliminary review by the New Paradigm Institute catalogues several batches: the first, received in January 1996, reportedly contained letters and three highly classified telegrams dated [13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }}) 1933; a second batch contained 1936 material concerning unidentified aircraft; and subsequent material included references to the supposed RS/33 organisation and a “Moretti Case”. The same review stresses an important limitation: although supporters reported period-consistent ink and paper tests, complete laboratory reports have not been published and the claimed forensic results have not received independent verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

For the narrower question of Vergiate, the important point is what this documentary collection apparently **does not do**. Published inventories and reproductions discuss an unidentified aircraft, secrecy, telegrams associated with Milan, [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) and later sightings, but the accessible descriptions do not contain a straightforward 13 June 1933 transport order saying that a recovered vehicle was to be taken to a SIAI hangar at Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

The explicit connection appears instead in Lissoni's later investigative account. In his presentation of the RS/33 case, he says that the newspaper *Il Giorno*, following information supplied by him, was the first to hypothesise that the supposed object recovered on 13 June 1933 had been hidden at the SIAI works at **“Vergiate or Sesto Calende”**, two neighbouring places in the province of Varese. Lissoni then states that he reached this identification from a series of clues which he believed fitted together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

That wording is unusually valuable because it shows the mechanism by which the location entered the story. It is an **identification by inference**. It also preserves geographical uncertainty that is frequently absent from modern summaries: the proposition initially concerned Vergiate **or** Sesto Calende, rather than an independently established Vergiate hangar.

## Why SIAI looked like the obvious place to search

The hypothesis was not geographically arbitrary. The district around Sesto Calende genuinely had an unusually strong aviation identity by 1933.

SIAI had been founded at Sesto Calende in 1915. Museo Agusta records that the company established its seaplane base at nearby Sant'Anna on Lake Maggiore, initially operating a flying school and manufacturing licence-built flying boats before developing its own designs. Alessandro Marchetti became technical director in 1922, and the company went on to produce aircraft including the celebrated S.55.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> Treccani likewise identifies Sesto Calende as SIAI's birthplace and confirms the company's importance between the wars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

There is unusually good evidence for the area's aviation importance in the exact year of the alleged incident. SIAI's S.55 flying boats were used in Italo Balbo's great formation flights, and the company's industrial history records that in 1933 a fleet of 24 S.55s made the second major transatlantic air cruise to North America.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> The municipality of Sesto Calende specifically states that the S.55X aircraft used in Balbo's celebrated 1933 flight were built at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comune di Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - For a researcher retrospectively asking where Fascist authorities might have taken an unusual aircraft recovered somewhere in Lombardy, this was therefore a compelling industrial landscape. Sesto Calende offered aircraft engineers, manufacturing facilities and an established relationship with military aviation. Sant'Anna provided an aviation facility on the water. Nearby Vergiate would later become an enormous aircraft-production centre.

Those real facts supplied the framework onto which the alleged recovery story could be mapped.

## The Milan clue narrowed the search towards Varese

Lissoni's first stated step was geographical. He reasoned that the supposed landing or recovery should be sought in the Milanese or wider Lombardy region because purported Stefani news-agency communications concerning the event originated through the Telegraph Office in Milan. From there, he turned towards the SIAI industrial district in Varese province.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

This reasoning is worth separating from the underlying documentary claim. A communication being transmitted from Milan would not, by itself, prove that the object described in it was stored near Milan. Administrative messages can originate away from the place they describe. Nevertheless, as an investigative lead it gave Lissoni a search radius.

Within that radius, Sesto Calende was an obvious aviation candidate. Vergiate lies immediately nearby, creating what later accounts could present as a compact industrial zone rather than three sharply differentiated historical sites: Sesto Calende, Sant'Anna and Vergiate.

That geographical compression became consequential. Once “the SIAI facilities near the alleged Lombardy recovery” became the working proposition, it was easy for the most impressive later SIAI site — Vergiate — to become the name attached to the entire hypothesis.

## Balbo supplied a second bridge to SIAI

Another important element was Italo Balbo. In the RS/33 narrative, Balbo is alleged to have been associated with the secret organisation supposedly established to investigate unconventional aircraft. Lissoni therefore regarded Balbo's genuine relationship with SIAI aviation as additional circumstantial support for the Varese connection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

The problem is evidential rather than geographical. The proposition effectively runs:

**Balbo was connected to SIAI aircraft; Balbo is alleged in the disputed material to have been connected with RS/33; therefore SIAI may have been involved in storing or studying the recovered object.**

The first relationship is historically demonstrable. The second belongs to the contested RS/33 material. The third is an inference connecting the two. None is equivalent to a contemporary record stating that Balbo ordered a recovered object delivered to Vergiate.

## The “Moretti Case” strengthened the regional hypothesis

A more intricate strand concerned a person named Moretti. Among the material associated with the RS/33 collection was a communication urging secrecy concerning what was called the “Moretti Case”. The New Paradigm Institute's inventory lists this as an undated Stefani-agency letter addressed to an “Alfredo”, advising silence about Moretti.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

Lissoni subsequently searched for a Moretti within the SIAI environment. His account says he found a Moretti listed among SIAI personnel in an employee publication, described as an official connected with the company's operational organisation. He then associated this individual with wartime records referring to a Moretti from Sesto Calende who had gone underground and with a theory that a fire had affected the hangar containing the alleged object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span>

This is one of the clearest examples of the methodology that produced the Vergiate identification. The sequence did not begin with a document saying “Moretti at Vergiate guarded the recovered craft”. Instead, researchers started with an unexplained surname in disputed material, searched the regional aviation industry for a matching person, and interpreted the match within the larger recovery hypothesis.

Such detective work can generate legitimate leads, but a name match is not documentary identification. “Moretti” is not itself enough to establish that the SIAI employee and the person referenced in the purported secret communication were the same individual, still less that he had custody of anomalous wreckage.

## The decisive problem: the Vergiate factory came later

The strongest independent check on the theory comes from industrial history, and it produces a significant chronological mismatch.

## Later geography was projected backwards onto 1933

The chronological mismatch also provides a plausible explanation for why Vergiate nevertheless became so convincing in later retellings.

From 1937 onwards, Vergiate really did become exactly the sort of place that sounds appropriate for secret aircraft work. The new factory had an adjoining flying field.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> Museo Agusta says its hangars subsequently produced thousands of aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> FlightGlobal describes a vast purpose-built structure capable of handling fixed-wing aircraft and notes its continuing aerospace use many decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightglobal.com/analysis/analysis-new-discipline-new-drive-at-leonardo-helicopters/127027.article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightglobal.com">[FlightGlobal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightglobal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ANALYSIS: New discipline, new drive at Leonardo Helicopters &#124; Analysis &#124; Flight GlobalFebruary 22, 2018...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 22, 2018</span></span></span>

The site's later importance was not temporary. After Agusta acquired control of SIAI in 1969, helicopter assembly was transferred to Vergiate, and the facility ultimately became a major helicopter final-assembly site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> To anyone investigating the case late in the twentieth century, “SIAI at Vergiate” therefore evoked an unmistakable image: enormous hangars, an airfield, restricted aerospace manufacturing and decades of military-industrial activity.

But that landscape is partly anachronistic when projected onto June 1933.

This is the central mechanism behind Vergiate's rise within the story. A genuine 1933 aviation centre at **Sesto Calende–Sant'Anna** became blended with the spectacular **Vergiate complex created from 1937 onwards**. Because all belonged to the same regional SIAI history, later narrators could treat them as essentially one continuous location even though their facilities and chronology differed.

## “Vergiate or Sesto Calende” became simply “Vergiate”

The evolution can be seen by comparing Lissoni's wording with later summaries.

His published online account explicitly says the alleged object was hypothesised to have been hidden at SIAI facilities in **“Vergiate or Sesto Calende”**. He also explicitly attributes the hypothesis to his own chain of matching clues.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span> A secondary Italian account reproducing the research likewise describes the identification as something the journalist reached by combining several elements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.silverland.info/documenti/gabinetto-rs-33/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: silverland.info">[Il mondo degli UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">silverland.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il mondo degli UFOGabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il mondo degli UFO...</span></span></span>

## Even the company name can conceal the chronology

There is a smaller but revealing historical compression in the phrase “SIAI-Marchetti factory at Vergiate in 1933”.

Treccani records that the company was founded in 1915 as Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia. In 1930 its formal name became Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia Savoia; a corporate name incorporating both aircraft and Savoia Marchetti dates to 1937, while the later SIAI-Marchetti designation followed in 1944.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

Alessandro Marchetti had been technical director since 1922 and his name was already closely associated with the company's aircraft, so using “SIAI-Marchetti” retrospectively is understandable shorthand.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> It nevertheless illustrates the same tendency found in the location claim: later elements of SIAI history are projected backwards to make a complicated industrial chronology sound like a single continuous entity.

This matters because the familiar phrase combines **a later company designation with a later Vergiate factory** and then places both in 1933. The underlying aviation company is real and was flourishing at the time; the precise formulation is historically less secure.

## What the evidence actually establishes about Vergiate

Three evidential layers can therefore be separated without taking a position on every aspect of the wider Magenta case.

The first is **well-established industrial history**. SIAI was an important aircraft manufacturer based at Sesto Calende before and during 1933. Sant'Anna formed part of that aviation landscape, and SIAI produced the S.55X aircraft associated with Balbo's celebrated 1933 transatlantic expedition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

The second is the **disputed documentary Magenta/RS-33 corpus**. Papers anonymously supplied to researchers from 1996 onwards purport to document secrecy surrounding unidentified aircraft and the activities of RS/33. The accessible evidential record does not provide independent archival provenance for the collection, while the laboratory tests invoked by supporters have not been fully published for independent assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

The third is the **Vergiate identification itself**. Lissoni's own account describes this as an inference assembled from geography, SIAI's aviation presence, Balbo and related clues, originally pointing to “Vergiate or Sesto Calende”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33...</span></span></span> Independent industrial histories then introduce a major qualification: the dedicated Vergiate factory was constructed only from 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[culturadimpresa.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span>

That combination produces a more precise conclusion than either unquestioning acceptance or blanket dismissal. The wider **SIAI/Sesto Calende connection** was a historically plausible avenue for investigators to explore because a major aircraft industry genuinely existed there in 1933. The specific **Vergiate storage claim**, however, is substantially weaker because the famous Vergiate facilities invoked to support it belong to a later phase of the company's development.

## Vergiate is a hypothesis that became part of the legend

The most useful way to understand Vergiate's place in the Magenta story is therefore as a case study in how historical reconstruction can become narrative certainty.

Researchers began with documents whose provenance remains disputed and which apparently did not directly identify the storage hangar. They then used genuine historical features of Lombardy's aviation landscape to fill that gap. SIAI was real; its Sesto Calende works were real; its relationship with Balbo's aviation programme was real; Sant'Anna was real; and, from 1937 onwards, the great Vergiate aircraft works were very real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

Those facts make the hypothesis understandable. They do not make every link in it documented.

The crucial chronological fact is that **1933 SIAI and later Vergiate are not interchangeable**. In the year of the alleged Magenta recovery, the company's established aviation centre was Sesto Calende and the associated Sant'Anna area. The purpose-built Vergiate factory that later came to dominate the company's physical identity was constructed four years afterwards.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span>

Consequently, the strongest evidence-based formulation is not that a UFO recovered [near Magenta]({{ 'near-magenta/' | relative_url }}) “was stored at Vergiate”. It is that later investigators, especially Alfredo Lissoni, **hypothesised a SIAI storage location in the Vergiate–Sesto Calende area from a network of geographical and biographical clues**, and subsequent retellings progressively shortened that qualified reconstruction to “Vergiate”. The independently documented history of SIAI explains why the theory seemed attractive, but it also exposes its most important weakness: the specific Vergiate factory now associated with the story belongs to 1937–38, not to June 1933.
