## Introduction

No strong industrial-history evidence supports the idea that Vergiate was already operating as SIAI’s purpose-built aircraft factory in June 1933. The best-documented chronology points instead to **[Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }}) and nearby Sant’Anna** as the company’s established manufacturing and seaplane centre at that date, while the dedicated [Vergiate works]({{ 'vergiate-works/' | relative_url }}) and adjoining flying field belong to a later expansion. Institutional and aviation-history sources repeatedly date the Vergiate site to **1937**, with the enormous factory building still associated with the site dated to **1938**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

That distinction creates a serious problem for the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) story. The claim concerns a recovery on [13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }}) 1933, but the familiar image of wreckage being concealed inside a large SIAI-Marchetti factory at Vergiate appears to place the event in industrial infrastructure that did not yet exist in its later form. This does not prove that nothing could have been hidden somewhere in SIAI’s wider network. It does mean that **“stored at the Vergiate aircraft factory in 1933” is historically anachronistic unless evidence for an earlier Vergiate facility can be produced**.

## What existed at Vergiate in 1933?

SIAI itself certainly existed in 1933. The company had been founded in 1915 at **Sesto Calende**, not Vergiate, and developed around the production of seaplanes. Museo Agusta places its early flying and manufacturing activity at Sesto Calende and the Sant’Anna seaplane base on Lake Maggiore. The Municipality of Sesto Calende likewise describes Sant’Anna as the former military seaplane base used for launching and recovering aircraft manufactured by SIAI in nearby works.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

This was no minor operation. By 1933 SIAI was a prominent Italian aircraft manufacturer, closely associated with Alessandro Marchetti and the S.55 flying boat. The importance of **Sesto Calende in precisely that year** is particularly useful evidence: a technical study of the S.55X cites a 1933 contemporary interview with Marchetti entitled *Sesto Calende e il 55 X*. Twenty-five S.55X aircraft were prepared for Italo Balbo's celebrated 1933 Atlantic formation flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.icas.org/icas_archive/ICAS2018/data/papers/ICAS2018_0437_paper.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: icas.org">[ICAS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">icas.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">REPLICA 55 PROJECT: A WOOD SEAPLANE IN THE ERA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alessandro Marchetti by Cesare Maffei,,Ala. Italiana magazine, 1933. [2] Perfetti A.: “In tema di Idrosilurante...Read more...</span></span></span>

In other words, the timing problem is not that SIAI lacked the industrial capacity to handle aircraft in 1933. It plainly had such capacity. The problem is geographical: **the documented 1933 SIAI aviation centre was the Sesto Calende–Sant’Anna complex, whereas the later landplane factory at Vergiate had not yet reached the form subsequently associated with the UFO claim**.

The company's formal name also changed over this period. Treccani records its foundation as Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia, its 1930 change to Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia Savoia, and a further 1937 change incorporating the Savoia-Marchetti designation. Thus the convenient modern phrase “SIAI-Marchetti factory at Vergiate” bundles together a company, name and industrial site whose histories did not all line up in that form in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

There is an important qualification. Sources concerning individual aircraft indicate aviation activity at Vergiate during the **mid-1930s**, before the large 1937–38 factory complex was completed. Sources disagree over some exact prototype dates: for example, accounts of the S.M.81 place its first flight from Vergiate in either 1934 or 1935.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/37516" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.pub">[encyclopedia.pub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti SM.811 Dec 2022 — 20099) first flew from Vergiate, near Varese, on 8 February 1934... Apart from its speed, it was gene...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 1934</span></span></span> This makes it unsafe to claim that Vergiate remained completely devoid of SIAI aviation activity until 1937. It does **not**, however, establish an operational Vergiate aircraft factory in June 1933. The distinction between an early flying ground or developmental activity and the later purpose-built manufacturing complex is crucial.

## When did the purpose-built factory and airfield appear?

The strongest sources converge around **1937–38** for the Vergiate industrial complex.

Museo Agusta, whose collection covers the history of both Agusta and SIAI-Marchetti, states that the [Vergiate hangars]({{ '1933-hangars/' | relative_url }}) had seen aircraft production since **1937**. A Politecnico di Milano document prepared in connection with a technical visit to the site likewise describes Vergiate as an important aviation location “since 1937”, when SIAI began fixed-wing production there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

More recent corporate material is equally explicit. A 2026 Leonardo information document describes the **Vergiate plant as founded in 1937**. That is particularly significant because Leonardo is the present industrial successor operating the site rather than a source concerned with the UFO controversy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.confindustriavarese.it/web_v4/site.nsf/dx/MediaInfoLDO_LOMBARDIA_giugno_2026.pdf/%24FILE/MediaInfoLDO_LOMBARDIA_giugno_2026.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: confindustriavarese.it">[Confindustria Varese]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">confindustriavarese.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">MediaInfoLDO LOMBARDIA giugno 2026</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Confindustria VareseLeonardo in LombardiaLo stabilimento di Vergiate, fondato nel 1937, è oggi — con circa 1.700 addetti — la principale...</span></span></span>

Industrial and architectural evidence narrows the chronology further. FlightGlobal describes the striking 365-metre assembly building still used at Vergiate as having been **built by SIAI Marchetti in 1938 as an aircraft factory**. An architectural-history document from Docomomo Italia similarly dates the great reinforced-concrete Vergiate hangar to **1938–39**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightglobal.com/analysis/analysis-new-discipline-new-drive-at-leonardo-helicopters/127027.article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightglobal.com">[FlightGlobal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightglobal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on flightglobal.com.</span></span></span>

Local industrial history also describes the development as an expansion rather than an already established factory. A history of SIAI in the 1930s records the beginning of work on a **new plant with an associated flying field at Vergiate**; another industrial-archaeology account says an additional production facility equipped with a flying field was created there beginning in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ilovesestocalende.it/gli-anni-trenta-in-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilovesestocalende.it">[IloveSestoCalende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilovesestocalende.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ilovesestocalende.it.</span></span></span>

There is unusually useful archival evidence for the site actually being used at that point. Fondazione Leonardo's SIAI-Marchetti historical archive contains a photograph catalogued as **“SM.75 aircraft – Vergiate Airport engine test 1937”**. Rather than relying only on later recollection, this gives a dated company-archive record placing SIAI aircraft activity at the Vergiate airfield in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lia.fondazioneleonardo.com/archivi/s/lia/item/24577" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lia.fondazioneleonardo.com">[LIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lia.fondazioneleonardo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">LIAVelivolo SM.75</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LIAVelivolo SM.75</span></span></span>

Taken together, the sources support a more careful chronology:

* **1933:** SIAI is an established aircraft manufacturer, centred historically on Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/c012120/zf/index.php/servizi-aggiuntivi/index/index/idtesto/108" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on va.it.</span></span></span>
* **Mid-1930s:** evidence begins to associate Vergiate with SIAI landplane testing or flying activity, although published accounts are inconsistent over individual dates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://encyclopedia.pub/entry/37516" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: encyclopedia.pub">[Encyclopedia.pub]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">encyclopedia.pub</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Savoia-Marchetti SM.811 Dec 2022 — 20099) first flew from Vergiate, near Varese, on 8 February 1934... * **1937:** multiple institutional and industrial histories date the dedicated Vergiate plant, production activity or major expansion to this year; the Leonardo archive independently records an SM.75 engine test at Vergiate in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.old.aero.polimi.it/uploads/media/20170912-15_ERF_general_information_programme.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: old.aero.polimi.it">[polimi.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">old.aero.polimi.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aero Welcome from the Organizing Committee</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Polimi AeroWelcome from the Organizing Committee...</span></span></span>
* **1938–39:** architectural sources date the enormous production building that became the site's defining industrial structure to this period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.flightglobal.com/analysis/analysis-new-discipline-new-drive-at-leonardo-helicopters/127027.article" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: flightglobal.com">[FlightGlobal]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">flightglobal.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on flightglobal.com.</span></span></span>

The safest conclusion is therefore not that “Vergiate had nothing to do with aviation before 1937”, but something narrower and better supported: **the major SIAI aircraft works later identified with Vergiate cannot simply be projected backwards to June 1933**.

## Why the construction date changes the UFO claim

The chronological problem becomes more important when the origin of the Vergiate identification is examined. One account published by the Italian UFO organisation CUN attributes the identification directly to researcher Roberto Pinotti. In that account, Pinotti says that the newspaper *Il Giorno*, acting on his indication, was the first to **hypothesise** that the alleged recovered object had been hidden at the SIAI facilities at “Vergiate or Sesto Calende”. He then explains the circumstantial reasoning that led him towards the Varese area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

That wording matters. Vergiate was not presented there as a location independently established by a contemporary 1933 transport record, warehouse inventory or factory document. It was an inference made later from the geography and SIAI connection. Indeed, the same account groups **Vergiate, Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna** together while describing the company's facilities, even though their functions and development dates were not identical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

Later retellings have tended to harden that inference into a much more definite statement. Contemporary popular accounts can simply say that the alleged vehicle was stored in SIAI-Marchetti's “hangars in Vergiate”, sometimes describing the location in ways that evoke the substantial later aircraft complex.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.wantedinrome.com/news/the-crash-that-predated-roswell-mussolinis-secret-ufo-files.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: wantedinrome.com">[Wanted in Rome]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">wantedinrome.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Wanted in Rome The Secret Crash That Predated Roswell: Mussolini&#x27;s UFO</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wanted in Rome The Secret Crash That Predated Roswell: Mussolini&#x27;s UFO</span></span></span> The industrial chronology shows why that wording needs qualification.

There are therefore three different propositions, and they should not be treated as equivalent. First, **SIAI possessed aircraft-manufacturing facilities in the wider area in 1933**: this is well supported, particularly for Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna. Second, **some form of SIAI-associated aviation activity may have developed at Vergiate during the mid-1930s before completion of the later factory**: there is evidence consistent with this, though the detailed chronology is uneven. Third, **a mature Vergiate aircraft factory with substantial hangars was available to conceal the alleged wreckage on 13 June 1933**: the industrial histories examined here do not support that proposition.

The third proposition is the one most relevant to the Magenta story as it is commonly visualised. The huge Vergiate works make an intuitively attractive secret-storage location because they really did become an important aircraft-production centre. But their later suitability cannot serve as evidence that the same infrastructure existed four years earlier.

## Could something still have been stored nearby?

The dating discrepancy does not justify the stronger conclusion that storage somewhere in the region was physically impossible. SIAI's established facilities at Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna make that argument untenable: there were genuine aviation workshops, industrial buildings and seaplane infrastructure within the company's local network in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/c012120/zf/index.php/servizi-aggiuntivi/index/index/idtesto/108" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Comune di Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on va.it.</span></span></span>

A revised version of the UFO claim could therefore substitute **Sesto Calende, Sant’Anna or an unidentified earlier building** for the later Vergiate factory. But doing so changes the evidential status of the story. It would be an attempt to repair the location hypothesis, not confirmation of the original Vergiate identification. No source establishing SIAI's genuine presence at Sesto Calende proves that anomalous wreckage was stored there.

This is especially important because the UFO literature itself leaves room for that uncertainty. The Pinotti account refers to “Vergiate or Sesto Calende” while explaining how the proposed location was inferred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ECN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span> The historical evidence therefore argues for preserving that uncertainty rather than converting it into the categorical statement that the object was taken to “the Vergiate factory”.

Nor does evidence of aircraft activity at Vergiate in 1934 or 1935 solve the problem automatically. A first flight from a field demonstrates that a usable flying ground existed by the date concerned; it does not retrospectively establish the presence in June 1933 of the large enclosed manufacturing and storage buildings constructed during the later expansion. The question is not merely whether an aeroplane could operate at Vergiate, but whether the **specific industrial storage environment invoked by the story existed at the required time**.

## The 1933 timing problem

On the available historical record, the answer to “Was Vergiate operating as an aircraft factory in 1933?” is therefore **probably not in the sense required by the familiar UFO-storage story**. SIAI was unquestionably operating in the region, but the documentary trail points towards its older Sesto Calende–Sant’Anna establishment in 1933 and towards the purpose-built Vergiate works emerging several years afterwards. Institutional, corporate, archival and architectural sources independently cluster around 1937–38 for the Vergiate plant and its defining factory infrastructure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[museoagusta.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

That does not disprove the alleged Magenta recovery itself, because a dating contradiction concerning one proposed storage location cannot establish what happened at another location. What it does undermine is a specific and frequently repeated piece of the narrative: **the picture of a recovered craft being delivered in June 1933 to the substantial SIAI-Marchetti aircraft factory at Vergiate known from later history**.

The most defensible reconstruction is more modest. In 1933, SIAI provided a very real aeronautical-industrial presence around Sesto Calende and Sant’Anna. Vergiate subsequently became the company's major land-aircraft production site, with documented activity by 1937 and its monumental factory building following around 1938.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/c012120/zf/index.php/servizi-aggiuntivi/index/index/idtesto/108" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[va.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on va.it.</span></span></span> The later Vergiate complex therefore fits the *idea* of a secret aviation storage site remarkably well — but its construction chronology makes it a poor fit for an event specifically dated to June 1933.
