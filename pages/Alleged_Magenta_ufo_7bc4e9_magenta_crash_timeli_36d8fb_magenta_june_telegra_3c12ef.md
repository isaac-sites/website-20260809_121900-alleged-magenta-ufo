## Introduction

The 13 June 1933 telegrams are the strongest reason that **13 June** became the standard date in the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) story. In the versions published by Roberto Pinotti and Alfredo Lissoni, the telegrams order officials and journalists to suppress reports of an aircraft of unknown nature or origin, refer to a Stefani news-agency dispatch issued earlier that day, and threaten serious penalties for disobedience. One circulated document explicitly invokes a personal order from Benito [Mussolini]({{ 'mussolini/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universonline.it/_misteri/articoli_u/articoli/00_04_18_a.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universonline.it">[Universo On Line]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universonline.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universo On Line Ufologia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universo On LineUfologia - E Mussolini nascose l&#x27;Ufo di Milano...</span></span></span>

But their evidential reach is much narrower than popular retellings suggest. Even if their wording is reproduced accurately, the papers do not themselves demonstrate an extraterrestrial craft, describe wreckage, identify Magenta as a crash site, or establish the elaborate later story of a recovered vehicle. More fundamentally, their [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) is unresolved: the purported Fascist files surfaced anonymously decades after the war rather than through a traceable state archive. An archival critique published by the Italian Centre for UFO Studies (CISU) argues that this missing chain of custody severely limits their historical value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? The useful question, therefore, is not simply whether the telegrams sound intriguing. It is **what would actually follow if their contents were accepted — and what still would not**.

## What the telegrams are said to contain

Published accounts generally describe three surviving telegrams connected with the events of [13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }}), while referring to a fourth communication that has not been produced. The papers are presented as highly confidential communications associated with Milan's telegraphic office and the Fascist information apparatus. Pinotti's own account says that there were three telegrams in hand out of four concerning the supposed recovery and that a protocol was intended for officials, intelligence services and newspapers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span>

The most frequently reproduced telegram contains the clearest claim. Dated **13 June 1933**, it says, in substance, that on Mussolini's personal order there must be absolute silence concerning the presumed landing on Italian soil of an unknown aircraft. It instructs recipients to adhere to the version distributed through the Stefani agency and warns that offenders could face severe punishment. A scan presented as this document is publicly available through Wikimedia Commons, which categorises it as a 1933 telegram associated with the alleged Fascist UFO material. The scan's circulation establishes what document proponents are referring to; it does not authenticate the document.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universonline.it/_misteri/articoli_u/articoli/00_04_18_a.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universonline.it">[Universo On Line]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universonline.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universo On Line Ufologia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universo On LineUfologia - E Mussolini nascose l&#x27;Ufo di Milano...</span></span></span>

A second reported telegram is potentially more interesting for reconstructing the internal chronology. Published transcriptions say it orders an immediate halt to dissemination of news concerning an aircraft of **unknown nature and origin** and refers back to a Stefani dispatch timed at **07:30 that morning**. It also reportedly orders the destruction or recasting of newspaper printing material carrying the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span></span></span>

A third telegram reportedly reiterates the censorship instructions. Italian press coverage of the papers has described the surviving set as three telegrams dated 13 June, with another, unrecovered telegram apparently referenced by them. That missing communication is important because some retellings treat what it supposedly contained as though the document itself were available. It is not: at that point the evidence becomes a reference **within the disputed documentary set**, rather than another independently inspectable record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span></span></span>

There is at least some historical plausibility in the institutional setting portrayed by the papers. Agenzia Stefani was not an invented organisation: under Manlio Morgagni it became the official news agency of the Fascist government. Likewise, the Special Tribunal for the Defence of the State was a real instrument of Fascist repression, established in 1926 and operating until Mussolini's fall in 1943. Thus references to state-controlled news and severe political sanctions belong to a recognisable Fascist environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/agenzia-stefani/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Agenzia stefani</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Agenzia stefani - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

That contextual fit matters, but it is only a consistency test. A document can employ historically appropriate institutions, vocabulary and practices without thereby proving that it was actually created by those institutions in 1933.

## Why 13 June is a documentary date, not automatically a crash date

The telegrams provide a relatively straightforward explanation for the prominence of **13 June 1933** in modern Magenta chronologies: that date is printed on the censorship material itself. The mistake comes when the date of the alleged communications is silently converted into the proven date of a crash.

The distinction is especially clear in the wording attributed to the best-known telegram. It reportedly refers to a **presumed landing** of an unknown aircraft. Another describes news relating to an aircraft of unknown nature and origin. Those propositions are considerably less specific than the later [Magenta narrative]({{ 'narrative-expansion/' | relative_url }}), in which a physical vehicle crashes, is recovered by military personnel and is eventually stored at an aircraft facility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universonline.it/_misteri/articoli_u/articoli/00_04_18_a.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universonline.it">[Universo On Line]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universonline.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universo On Line Ufologia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universo On LineUfologia - E Mussolini nascose l&#x27;Ufo di Milano...</span></span></span>

Even on the assumption that the telegrams are authentic, a censorship order dated 13 June proves at most that an authority was issuing an order on **13 June** about a reported event. It does not logically require that the underlying incident happened that same day. The event could have occurred earlier, the report could have been erroneous, or officials could have been reacting to a rumour whose factual basis was uncertain.

The supposed 07:30 Stefani dispatch makes this distinction more important, not less. If genuine, the reference would show that information about an unidentified aircraft was already circulating through the official news system early on 13 June. It would be useful evidence for the **information timeline**. It would not, without the missing source dispatch or another contemporary record, tell us exactly when, where or under what circumstances the reported aircraft came to official attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ilgiorno.it/milano/cultura/ufo-milano-fascisti-b311af53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ilgiorno.it">[Il Giorno]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ilgiorno.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Giorno Quell’Ufo caduto a Milano e subito nascosto dai fascisti</span></span></span>

This is why the telegrams support a **June documentary chronology** much more securely than they support a precise June crash chronology.

## The provenance problem

The largest obstacle is not an unusual word or an implausible bureaucratic reference. It is the way the alleged documents entered the historical record.

The Fascist UFO papers did not emerge from a catalogue entry in the Italian state archives accompanied by a file number, originating office, register and documented custody history. Pinotti describes the material as arriving through the post beginning in 1996 from an anonymous source. Later batches and reproductions were incorporated into what became known as the Fascist UFO Files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span>

That distinction is fundamental in archival research. Massimiliano Grandi, an archivist writing for CISU, argued that the papers could not safely be treated as official historical documents because their originating archive was unknown and their provenance could not be verified. His assessment was informed by discussion with Paola Carucci, a former senior official of the Italian archival administration and professor of archival studies. The critique also stressed limited access to the material for independent examination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

Grandi identified another complication: the alleged collection is not physically uniform. According to his reconstruction, some early items were said to have been received as originals, whereas much of the later material circulated as colour photocopies. A photocopy cannot normally answer many of the physical questions that an original can — about paper, ink, impressions, alterations, stamps and other characteristics relevant to [authentication]({{ 'authentication/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

Supporters have offered a different assessment. Pinotti says that chemical examination by Antonio Garavaglia found the paper and ink of originals consistent with Fascist-era material and argues that the results support authenticity. His published account also says that comparisons with other historical documents favoured the same conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span>

Yet even accepting such dating tests at face value would not solve the central provenance problem. Showing that a piece of paper and ink could date from the 1930s is not identical to demonstrating that **a particular government office created a particular text on 13 June 1933**. Old stationery can survive; contemporary-looking text can be placed on old material; and physical compatibility cannot reconstruct a missing chain of custody.

This is why the disagreement cannot fairly be reduced to “tests proved the telegrams genuine” versus “sceptics say they are fake”. There are several separate questions: whether the physical materials are old, whether the text was placed on them in the 1930s, whether the bureaucratic form is authentic, whether the issuing office genuinely produced them, and whether the events described actually happened. Evidence bearing on one stage does not automatically establish the others.

## The strongest inference is censorship, not a UFO recovery

Suppose, for analytical purposes, that the 13 June telegrams were authenticated beyond reasonable doubt. What would change?

Quite a lot — but less than the full Magenta story requires.

They would provide significant contemporary evidence that Fascist authorities were concerned about reports of an unidentified aircraft and wanted information about it suppressed. The repeated references to restricting press dissemination would also fit the broader political environment in which Agenzia Stefani operated as the regime's official news agency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/agenzia-stefani/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Agenzia stefani</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Agenzia stefani - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

The reported threat of referral to the Special Tribunal would likewise make sense within the machinery of dictatorship. The Archivio Centrale dello Stato describes that tribunal as one of the central instruments of the special legislation through which Fascism tightened political and social control after 1926. In other words, an authentic censorship order using the threat of state-security punishment would not itself be extraordinary for Mussolini's Italy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://search.acs.beniculturali.it/OpacACS/guida/IT-ACS-AS0001-0003603" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: search.acs.beniculturali.it">[ACS Beniculturali]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">search.acs.beniculturali.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ACS Beniculturali Archivio Centrale dello Stato</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ACS BeniculturaliArchivio Centrale dello Stato - Guida ai Fondi - TRIBUNALE SPECIALE PER LA DIFESA DELLO STATO (1925-1945)...</span></span></span>

What would remain unproved is much more specific:

* **A crash at Magenta.** The reproduced telegram language refers to a presumed landing or an aircraft, not a documented wreck site at Magenta.
* **An extraterrestrial origin.** “Unknown” nature or provenance means unidentified in the reported information; it does not identify the object as non-human.
* **Recovery of a spacecraft.** The core censorship wording does not supply a technical recovery report, inventory of wreckage or engineering description.
* **The later physical descriptions.** Familiar claims about a bell-, acorn- or disc-shaped vehicle are not established by these censorship orders.
* **Bodies or occupants.** The telegrams do not provide evidence for the later stories of humanoid occupants.
* **Storage at Vergiate.** Establishing a censorship order would not independently establish the claimed movement of a wreck to SIAI-Marchetti facilities.
* **The entire [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) narrative.** Authentication of one group of papers would not automatically authenticate every other document subsequently attributed to the alleged secret organisation.

That last point is especially important because popular summaries often treat the Fascist UFO Files as though they were one archival dossier discovered intact. The published history is instead one of material arriving in different instalments from an unidentified source, with originals and reproductions carrying different evidential limitations. CISU's archival critique specifically warns against assigning the whole collection the status of verified official records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? ## What would actually strengthen the telegram case?

The debate sometimes concentrates heavily on typography, wording or whether the papers “look old”. Those questions matter, but the decisive evidence would be external corroboration.

The strongest development would be discovery of a matching copy, registry entry or referenced dispatch in a recognised archive with an independent chain of custody. The alleged **07:30 Stefani dispatch** would be particularly valuable. If a securely archived copy existed and matched the references in the later censorship telegrams, it would create an independent documentary bridge into the official communications system of 13 June 1933.

Likewise, a government register recording the telegram's transmission, a recipient's archived copy, contemporary correspondence discussing the suppression order, or newspaper-production records documenting the withdrawal of an already prepared story would materially strengthen the case. Such evidence would be powerful precisely because it would not depend upon the anonymous 1990s source.

Conversely, simply finding genuine Fascist documents that resemble the telegrams would have limited value. Historical consistency establishes that a disputed paper *could* fit its claimed period; archival linkage is what helps establish that it actually belongs there.

This distinction also explains why the real existence of Agenzia Stefani and the Special Tribunal should be treated cautiously. Both institutions unquestionably existed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/agenzia-stefani/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Agenzia stefani</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Agenzia stefani - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span> Their presence in the telegram narrative makes the text historically intelligible, but does not independently authenticate the particular communications attributed to them.

## What do the 13 June telegrams prove?

At present, the safest conclusion has three levels.

**First, they establish the source of the modern 13 June chronology.** There really are circulated documents presented as Fascist telegrams dated 13 June 1933, and published accounts reproduce their alleged censorship instructions. The June date is therefore not merely a recent internet embellishment; it is embedded in the disputed documentary tradition on which the modern case was built.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATelegramma_Mussolini_13_giugno_1933.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg - Wikimedia Commons...</span></span></span>

**Second, their contents, if authentic, would establish an official secrecy episode involving reports of an unidentified aircraft.** References to Mussolini, Agenzia Stefani, suppression of newspaper coverage and punishment of those spreading the story would make the papers significant evidence about what Fascist authorities believed or wanted concealed. They would still be evidence of a government reaction to a report, not direct [physical evidence]({{ 'physical-evidence/' | relative_url }}) of what generated that report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.universonline.it/_misteri/articoli_u/articoli/00_04_18_a.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: universonline.it">[Universo On Line]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">universonline.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Universo On Line Ufologia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Universo On LineUfologia - E Mussolini nascose l&#x27;Ufo di Milano...</span></span></span>

**Third, their current provenance prevents that conditional finding from becoming a secure historical conclusion.** CISU's archival criticism goes directly to this point: the documents arrived anonymously, their archival origin could not be identified, some important material circulated as reproductions, and independent access was contested. Claims of physical testing favourable to a 1930s date are relevant but do not reconstruct the missing custodial history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The resulting distinction is narrow but crucial for the disputed Magenta timeline. **13 June 1933 is well anchored as the date written on the alleged censorship telegrams. It is not thereby independently established as the date on which an extraterrestrial vehicle crashed at Magenta.** The telegrams are best understood as disputed documentary evidence for a suppression story — potentially important if authenticated, but incapable on their own of carrying the much larger historical claim that has subsequently been built around them.
