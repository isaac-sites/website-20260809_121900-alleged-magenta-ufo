## Introduction

The papers behind the [Gabinetto]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) RS/33 story have a fundamental evidential weakness: they did not emerge from a Fascist-era government archive with a documented chain of custody. They arrived anonymously through the post in the 1990s, more than six decades after the events they purportedly describe. The unidentified sender became known as “Mister X”, and the [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) were publicised chiefly by Italian UFO researchers Roberto Pinotti and Alfredo Lissoni.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ecn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>



<figure class="article-media-figure article-media-figure-illustration"><img src="{{ "/assets/images/Alleged_Magenta_ufo_7bc4e9_gabinetto_rs33_7ae6cf_rs33_anonymous_paper_f75674-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Anonymous Papers illustration 1" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Alleged_Magenta_ufo_7bc4e9_gabinetto_rs33_7ae6cf_rs33_anonymous_paper_f75674-Illustration-1-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Alleged_Magenta_ufo_7bc4e9_gabinetto_rs33_7ae6cf_rs33_anonymous_paper_f75674-Illustration-1-light.svg" | relative_url }}" width="1920" height="1080" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low"><figcaption class="article-media-caption"><span class="article-media-caption-label">Explanatory illustration 1</span></figcaption></figure>

That does not automatically make the papers fraudulent. Some reportedly use period stationery and materials judged compatible with the 1930s. But compatibility of paper, ink or handwriting with an era is a much narrower finding than proof that a document was actually created by Mussolini's government, circulated through the offices it names and preserved continuously since then. The crucial [missing evidence]({{ 'missing-evidence/' | relative_url }}) is [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}): a traceable history connecting the papers to their alleged institutional creators. Archival authorities define provenance in terms that include precisely this chain of ownership and custody.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/reference/archives-resources/terminology.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesArchives and Records Management Resources &#124; National ArchivesJanuary 1, 1984...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1984</span></span></span>

For the RS/33 claim, that distinction is decisive. The anonymous papers are not merely supporting evidence for the secret organisation; they are the principal reason modern researchers believe such an organisation existed at all.

## How the Mister X papers entered the story

The modern documentary trail begins in 1996. According to accounts by Pinotti and later researchers, an [anonymous source]({{ 'anonymous-source/' | relative_url }}) sent material suggesting that Fascist authorities had dealt secretly with unusual aircraft during the 1930s. An early packet reportedly contained two letters on Senate stationery and three telegrams dated [13 June]({{ '13-june/' | relative_url }}) 1933. One of the letters referred to the body later identified as Gabinetto RS/33, while the telegrams appeared to record urgent government communications surrounding the alleged Lombardy incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oggiscienza.it/2020/08/13/x-files-fascisti/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oggiscienza.it">[oggiscienza.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oggiscienza.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1933, quando un ufo cadde in Lombardia &#124; OggiScienza...</span></span></span>

Further material followed. Accounts of the collection describe a handwritten memorandum concerning unusual objects seen over Mestre in 1936, a telegram associated with the Stefani news agency demanding secrecy concerning unidentified aircraft, correspondence referring to the supposed RS/33 organisation and other miscellaneous pieces. The collection was therefore not one recovered administrative file but an assortment of purported documents associated retrospectively with the same hidden programme.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm Institute...</span></span></span>

The sender's identity was never established publicly. UFO researchers nicknamed the source “[Mister X]({{ 'mister-x/' | relative_url }})”. Later descriptions said the sender claimed access through a family connection to somebody involved with the alleged organisation. The Centro Ufologico Nazionale (CUN), which promotes the case, now describes the source as an anonymous descendant of an RS/33 member. Whatever the precise version, the central evidential fact remains unchanged: there is no publicly documented transfer from an Italian government repository to the researchers who received the papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.eu/rivelazioni-di-david-grusch-sui-file-fascisti-comunicato-stampa-cun/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.eu">[CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALERivelazioni di David Grusch sui file fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comunicato Stampa CUN - CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALEJune 13, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2023</span></span></span>

The material also circulated outside a conventional archival route. Contemporary accounts say copies or related packages went to UFO organisations, researchers and publications. Lissoni wrote that the documents had been sent anonymously to the CUN and other associations, while later reporting noted that newspapers also received material but did not pursue it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ecn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

That history matters because publication does not establish origin. Once anonymous documents enter circulation through magazines, private collections and photocopies, researchers can study what is written on them, their physical characteristics where originals survive, and their internal consistency. What becomes much harder to reconstruct afterwards is the most important historical question: **where exactly were these papers between the 1930s and the 1990s?**

## What provenance would normally establish

For historians and archivists, provenance is more than knowing who happened to possess a document most recently. The US National Archives defines it both as the principle of keeping records associated with their creating records unit and as information about a record's chain of ownership and custody. The Library of Congress likewise treats custodial history as information about ownership or possession before material reaches a repository, noting that such history can affect assessments of authority, integrity and interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.archives.gov/research/alic/reference/archives-resources/terminology.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archives.gov">[National Archives]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archives.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">National ArchivesArchives and Records Management Resources &#124; National ArchivesJanuary 1, 1984...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1984</span></span></span>

Applied to RS/33, a strong provenance case would therefore need evidence connecting the purported papers backwards through identifiable custodians to the Fascist institutions that supposedly generated or received them. Useful anchors could include an original file series, registry entries, protocol numbers, contemporary routing marks, associated correspondence, acquisition records or independently preserved duplicates in another official collection.

The anonymous RS/33 papers have not been shown to possess that kind of institutional history. Reporting critical of the case notes that the controversial documents lack such formal archival identifiers as protocol numbers and verifiable archival markings and resemble private correspondence more than records recovered from an official file. OggiScienza similarly concluded that even pieces on official-looking stationery had not been demonstrated to belong to a public archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia...</span></span></span>

This distinction becomes clearer when the Mister X material is compared with genuine contemporary records that **are** held in an archive. Research into the Prefecture of Milan uncovered files concerning suspicious aircraft, airspace violations and unidentified aeroplanes from the 1930s. These records sit within an identifiable institutional collection rather than an anonymous postal delivery. The Italian State Archives in Milan publishes inventories for the Prefecture holdings, demonstrating the sort of fonds-and-file context against which government records can normally be evaluated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://archiviodistatomilano.cultura.gov.it/patrimonio/fondi-e-inventari/q" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: archiviodistatomilano.cultura.gov.it">[Archivio di Stato di Milano]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">archiviodistatomilano.cultura.gov.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Archivio di Stato di MilanoArchivio di Stato di Milano: QMay 8, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 8, 2026</span></span></span>

Crucially, even the CUN-linked account acknowledges that these genuine archival records do not mention the purported Milan RS/33 files. They record a real Fascist surveillance system dealing with suspicious aircraft, including many incidents eventually attributable to ordinary foreign, civilian, smuggling or reconnaissance aircraft. Their existence demonstrates that Fascist authorities monitored unidentified aircraft; it does **not** independently authenticate the anonymous RS/33 papers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ecn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

That is an important evidential boundary. An authentic 1933 telegram about an unidentified aircraft can corroborate the existence of an air-defence concern on that date without corroborating a separate anonymous document claiming a secret organisation investigated recovered exotic technology.

## Why old-looking paper cannot prove institutional origin

Supporters of the RS/33 papers have placed considerable weight on forensic examination. The CUN says consultant Antonio Garavaglia examined an original handwritten document on Senate stationery dated 22 August 1936 and regards the analysis as confirmation that the material is authentic and of the period. The document concerned an unusual aerial sighting over Mestre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/fafil.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">files fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">files fascisti</span></span></span>

That claim is significant, but its evidential reach needs to be kept precise. Reporting on the examination has noted criticism that the published material does not adequately explain the methods by which authenticity was established. OggiScienza describes it as a commissioned examination and reports continuing objections to treating the result as proof of the larger historical narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[VICE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia...</span></span></span>

More fundamentally, forensic compatibility and archival authenticity answer different questions. Document examination can investigate such things as paper composition, watermarks, printing, writing materials, alterations and ink characteristics. A US Department of Justice reference on questioned-document dating stresses that investigators should also establish the circumstances of a document's production, history and discovery, and notes limitations in determining ink age.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ojp.gov/ncjrs/virtual-library/abstracts/age-document" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ojp.gov">[Office of Justice Programs]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ojp.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Office of Justice Programs AGE OF A DOCUMENT &#124; Office of Justice Programs</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Office of Justice Programs AGE OF A DOCUMENT &#124; Office of Justice Programs</span></span></span>

Suppose, for example, that an RS/33 sheet could be demonstrated conclusively to have been manufactured in 1933. That would rule out a forgery made on paper manufactured decades later. It would **not**, by itself, demonstrate that the text was written in 1933, that the named government office produced it, that Mussolini authorised its contents or that the sheet had remained in official custody.

The same problem applies to period letterheads and stationery. Genuine unused paper can survive for decades. A document created later on old stock can therefore contain authentic period material while carrying inauthentic text. Conversely, a genuinely old private note might accurately date from the 1930s yet still not constitute an official state record. Physical age is consequently one component of authentication, not a substitute for provenance.

This is why the anonymous origin cannot be repaired simply by showing that a sheet “looks right”. For the RS/33 case, the strongest possible physical examination would ideally be combined with an identifiable creator, comparison against authenticated records from the same office, documentary evidence of circulation and a traceable [custody history]({{ 'custody-history/' | relative_url }}). Without those links, laboratory compatibility narrows the possible explanations but does not establish the claimed institutional origin.

## The archive search produced a revealing contrast

One of the most useful developments in the RS/33 investigation was the discovery of genuine Fascist-era material in Milan — precisely because those documents illustrate what independently recoverable evidence looks like.

Lissoni reported examining hundreds of telegrams and telephone messages concerning suspicious or unidentified aircraft in the Prefecture archives. Some described aircraft that observers initially could not identify; others were subsequently recognised as Swiss, French, German, Dutch or other conventional aircraft. The records document a surveillance network involving prefectural offices, airfields, police and other authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ecn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

There were even reports close to the key date in the Magenta story. The published CUN investigation lists reports of unknown or suspicious aircraft in northern Italy during June 1933, including entries on 3, 8 and 13 June. But the same investigation concedes that these archival papers do not mention the disputed Milan RS/33 files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ecn.org/cunfi/rs33.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ecn.org">[ecn.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ecn.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Il Gabinetto RS-33</span></span></span>

That creates an instructive asymmetry. The ordinary air-surveillance system leaves records that can be located in an established state collection. The extraordinary organisation allegedly created to handle the recovered object has, so far, left no comparably authenticated institutional trail.

Absence from surviving archives cannot prove that RS/33 never existed. Secret organisations can produce incomplete records; wartime destruction, deliberate disposal and administrative upheaval can remove documentation. But secrecy does not make provenance irrelevant. If anything, a claim involving a deliberately hidden state programme requires especially strong independent links because otherwise every missing record can be explained as another consequence of secrecy.

The anonymous papers therefore cannot gain authenticity merely because authentic archives demonstrate that the Fascist state monitored its airspace. The latter fact makes the setting historically plausible while leaving the specific RS/33 proposition unverified.

## The source problem cannot be separated from the documents

“Mister X” is sometimes treated as though anonymity were simply an unfortunate obstacle surrounding otherwise self-authenticating records. In reality, the unknown source and the uncertain documents form one evidential problem.

If the sender had been identified, researchers could test the claimed route by which the papers survived. A family archive might contain other records from the same official, photographs, diaries, appointment papers or correspondence demonstrating that the alleged custodian actually occupied the position required by the story. Researchers could establish when the documents changed hands and whether other family members remembered them. None of those checks is possible to the same degree while the source remains unidentified.

The CUN's own historical investigation recognised the problem strongly enough to appeal publicly for Mister X to come forward. OggiScienza later repeated that appeal, arguing that the sender's identity and explanation of how the papers were obtained remained necessary to resolving the dispute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oggiscienza.it/2020/08/13/x-files-fascisti/index.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oggiscienza.it">[oggiscienza.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oggiscienza.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">1933, quando un ufo cadde in Lombardia &#124; OggiScienza...</span></span></span>

There is also a circularity risk. The documents are used to establish RS/33; the anonymous source is then described as a descendant or associate of somebody connected with RS/33; that supposed connection is in turn used to strengthen confidence in the documents. Unless the source's claimed relationship can be independently established, neither proposition independently verifies the other.

This is why anonymity matters more here than it would for a leaked document whose contents could readily be checked against other evidence. If an anonymous memorandum accurately predicts facts independently confirmed in multiple government archives, its unknown origin becomes less damaging. In the RS/33 case, however, many of the narrative's most distinctive propositions — the organisation's name, secret remit and connection with unusual aircraft — derive primarily from the disputed material itself.

## Can the anonymous RS/33 papers be trusted?

The most defensible assessment is that they can be treated as **artefacts underlying the modern RS/33 story, but not yet as authenticated records of a Fascist government programme**.

There are reasons not to dismiss them solely on appearance. Some material was reportedly supplied in original form rather than only as photocopies; period stationery and physical characteristics have been cited in their favour; and archival research has confirmed that Fascist authorities really did collect reports of suspicious and unidentified aircraft. The CUN therefore argues that the papers are genuine historical documents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.eu/rivelazioni-di-david-grusch-sui-file-fascisti-comunicato-stampa-cun/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.eu">[CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALERivelazioni di David Grusch sui file fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comunicato Stampa CUN - CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALEJune 13, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2023</span></span></span>

Against that stands a more consequential set of deficiencies. The crucial papers arrived anonymously in the 1990s. No publicly demonstrated Fascist-era chain of custody connects them to the offices they purport to represent. The forensic examination most frequently cited in their favour has not produced a broadly accepted independent authentication of the entire collection. And genuine government records located in the Milan archives establish contemporary aircraft surveillance without independently establishing Gabinetto RS/33.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.vice.com/it/article/x-files-fascisti-primo-avvistamento-ufo-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: vice.com">[vice.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">vice.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gli &#x27;X-Files fascisti&#x27; e la strana teoria del primo avvistamento Ufo in Italia...</span></span></span>

A recent publication hosted in the Italian National Research Council's repository illustrates how far the claim remains from institutional acceptance: in discussing legends attached to Guglielmo Marconi, it characterises the documents connecting the supposed RS/33 group with exotic technology as being of more than doubtful origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://iris.cnr.it/bitstream/20.500.14243/554086/3/Ferro_GALILEO_2024_Comunicazioni.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: iris.cnr.it">[IRIS]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">iris.cnr.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">IRISDalla clava al wireless…</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">IRISDalla clava al wireless…</span></span></span>

What would materially change the assessment is straightforward: identification of the original custodian; a documented custody history extending back towards the 1930s; discovery of matching RS/33 records in an established state or personal archive; registry or protocol evidence showing that the disputed papers actually circulated through the offices named on them; or independent forensic examinations of the surviving originals with published methods and results.

Until evidence of that kind appears, the anonymous papers remain the central evidence for RS/33 and simultaneously its central weakness. They show how the modern claim was constructed, but they have not yet supplied the archival bridge required to turn that claim into established history.
