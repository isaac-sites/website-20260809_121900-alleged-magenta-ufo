## Introduction

Vergiate is best understood as the **alleged storage destination in the 1933 Magenta UFO story, not as a securely identified crash site**. In Roberto Pinotti's reconstruction, the unknown object was recovered elsewhere in Lombardy—usually described as [near Magenta]({{ 'near-magenta/' | relative_url }})—and was subsequently concealed at aviation facilities associated with SIAI. Pinotti himself presented Vergiate or neighbouring [Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }}) as an identification reached through later investigation rather than as a location explicitly established by a contemporary crash record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/gabinetto%20rs33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNOPENE D FILE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNOPENE D FILE</span></span></span>

That distinction becomes more important when the chronology of the real aviation facilities is checked. Authoritative histories date the construction of SIAI's new Vergiate factory to **1937**, four years after the alleged 1933 incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> The historical aviation connection is therefore genuine, but the familiar claim that an object recovered in 1933 was immediately placed in the later Vergiate factory's hangars contains a chronological problem. It also helps explain why accounts that simply call Vergiate "the crash site" compress several different stages of the legend into one place.

## How Vergiate enters the standard reconstruction

The most useful source for understanding Vergiate's role is [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }})'s own account of how he arrived at it. In material published by the Centro Ufologico Nazionale, he describes the object as having been recovered by the Fascist authorities and then hidden at SIAI facilities at **Vergiate or Sesto Calende**. More significantly, he explains that this was an inference: he says the newspaper *Il Giorno*, acting on his indication, was the first to propose the SIAI connection, and then lists the circumstantial considerations that led him towards the area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/gabinetto%20rs33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNOPENE D FILE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNOPENE D FILE</span></span></span>

His reasoning connected several facts. The alleged messages surrounding the recovery were associated with Milan; the incident was therefore presumed to have occurred somewhere in Lombardy or the Milan region. SIAI was a major aviation manufacturer with operations around Sesto Calende and, subsequently, Vergiate, providing the kind of technical and controlled environment that could plausibly feature in a story about concealing an unusual aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/gabinetto%20rs33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNOPENE D FILE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNOPENE D FILE</span></span></span>

That is materially different from having a contemporary document saying, in effect, "take the recovered object to Vergiate". The publicly available case rests on anonymously supplied material that surfaced decades later, and critics have challenged both its [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}) and the conclusions subsequently built around it. A critical assessment by the Italian Center for Ufological Studies, CISU, argues that the so-called Fascist UFO files have serious documentary and archival weaknesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2023/08/the-alleged-1933-italian-ufo-crash.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the alleged 1933 italian ufo crash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesThe Alleged 1933 Italian UFO Crash Under Scrutiny – Fascist UFO FilesAugust 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2023</span></span></span>

The distinction can therefore be expressed quite simply: **Vergiate belongs to the reconstruction of what allegedly happened after recovery**. It should not automatically be treated as evidence for where the object originally came down.

## The claimed transfer from the Magenta area

In the version most commonly associated with Pinotti's research, the geographical sequence has two separate stages:

1. an unidentified object allegedly comes down or is recovered in the broader Magenta/Lombardy area;
2. the recovered material is supposedly moved into the SIAI aviation environment for concealment and investigation.

The Black Vault's reproduction of Pinotti's account preserves precisely this division: it describes the event as occurring near Magenta and says that the object was **later stored** in SIAI hangars at Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span> A later review by the New Paradigm Institute similarly treats Magenta as the alleged recovery location while discussing the subsequent handling of the object as a separate stage, although it stresses the lack of independent archival corroboration for the claimed secret organisation behind the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

This is why "Magenta versus Vergiate" can be a misleading formulation if treated as a simple choice between two rival crash coordinates. Within the standard reconstruction, they perform different narrative functions. **Magenta identifies the alleged incident or recovery area; Vergiate identifies the alleged place of concealment.**

The distinction is reinforced by the wording used in accounts sympathetic to the story. Even a Brazilian UFO publication summarising Pinotti's position says that the supposed crash site was near Magenta and that he identified the SIAI facility at Vergiate as the probable place where the alleged wreckage was subsequently stored.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufo.com.br/documentos-comprovam-queda-nave/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufo.com.br">[UFO]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufo.com.br</span><span class="citation-popover-title">documentos comprovam queda nave</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Documentos extraordinários comprovam queda de nave alienígena em 1933 – UFOJuly 3, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 3, 2023</span></span></span> Such sources cannot independently prove the claim, but they are useful for tracing what the claim actually is.

There is, however, a major historical complication.

## The 1937 factory date complicates the story

Vergiate unquestionably became an important SIAI aviation centre. The difficulty is **when**.

The Centro per la cultura d'impresa's institutional history of SIAI states that in **1937** the company built a new factory at Vergiate, a few kilometres from Sesto Calende, together with an adjoining airfield.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> Italy's Ministry of Defence likewise says that the Vergiate plant originated in 1937, while historical material from the Agusta Museum describes the same [Vergiate hangars]({{ '1933-hangars/' | relative_url }}) as dating from that year.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.difesa.it/smd/news-italia/nato-framework-nations-concept-italiano-visita-stabilimenti-leonardo/52201.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on difesa.it.</span></span></span>

That creates a straightforward chronological problem for literal versions of the UFO story. If the alleged recovery occurred in 1933, the object could not in that year have been placed in a Vergiate factory that historical sources say was constructed in 1937.

This does **not**, by itself, disprove every conceivable version of a transfer to the wider SIAI organisation. SIAI itself was much older. It had been founded in 1915 at Sesto Calende, and its aviation activities around Sesto Calende and the Sant'Anna seaplane base were firmly established well before 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> Treccani likewise identifies Sesto Calende as the company's original base and describes SIAI as an important inter-war aircraft manufacturer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

It does, however, mean that three propositions should not be casually treated as interchangeable:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* concealment somewhere within the **SIAI organisation** after 1933;
* concealment at **Sesto Calende or another existing SIAI facility**;
* concealment specifically in the **Vergiate factory and hangars**.

Only the first two avoid the obvious problem created by the documented 1937 opening of the [Vergiate works]({{ 'vergiate-works/' | relative_url }}). A modified version could posit initial storage elsewhere followed by a later move to Vergiate, but that would itself require evidence. The publicly available sources reviewed here do not establish such a transfer with an authenticated contemporary inventory, transport order or factory record.

## Why Vergiate nevertheless became convincing in later accounts

The enduring attraction of Vergiate is understandable because its later history fits the *type* of place that a secret-aircraft narrative requires remarkably well.

By the late 1930s it really was a major aviation manufacturing location. SIAI's inter-war aircraft included internationally prominent designs, and the company became an important producer of land-based military aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span> The Vergiate works had their own flying field and eventually became deeply embedded in Italian aerospace production. The Ministry of Defence still describes the site as an important final-assembly centre in the lineage that ultimately passed into Leonardo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.difesa.it/smd/news-italia/nato-framework-nations-concept-italiano-visita-stabilimenti-leonardo/52201.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: difesa.it">[difesa.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">difesa.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on difesa.it.</span></span></span>

For a retrospective UFO reconstruction, that creates an intuitively persuasive setting: large hangars, engineers, aircraft manufacturing, restricted industrial areas and military connections. Yet those characteristics establish **plausibility as a later storage setting**, not evidence that an anomalous object was actually there.

Indeed, Pinotti's published reasoning illustrates the difference particularly clearly. His identification of the SIAI area arose from connecting geographical and aviation clues rather than from producing a verified 1933 SIAI document naming a specific building.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/gabinetto%20rs33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNOPENE D FILE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNOPENE D FILE</span></span></span> Once that inferential conclusion entered UFO literature, however, subsequent retellings often presented "the SIAI hangars at Vergiate" as an established fact rather than as the endpoint of Pinotti's reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

Vergiate's genuine aerospace history thus gives the story a powerful historical anchor while also making it easy to overlook the gap between **documented aviation history** and **alleged UFO storage history**.

## Why later retellings blur recovery and storage

Geographical drift is visible across modern accounts. Some sources preserve the original two-stage structure—Magenta for the alleged recovery and Vergiate for later storage—while others move the entire event to Vergiate.

One recent Italian article, for example, describes the UFO as having crashed at Vergiate itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cultweb.it/cultura/misteri/lufo-che-spavento-mussolini-la-storia-di-un-mistero-da-vergiate-agli-usa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cultweb.it">[CultWeb.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cultweb.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cultweb.it.</span></span></span> Other summaries describe a crash somewhere between Milan and Varese followed by transport of the vehicle to Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufopedia.it/Gabinetto_RS/33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufopedia.it">[Ufopedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufopedia.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gabinetto RS/33</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gabinetto RS/33</span></span></span> Still others retain the clearer formula that it crashed near Magenta and was subsequently stored in Vergiate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case Files...</span></span></span>

The confusion probably has several reinforcing causes. Vergiate is the more tangible aviation location, so writers naturally attach the whole incident to the place where the supposed object could most readily be imagined inside a hangar. "Magenta" also became shorthand for the entire case, especially in English-language discussion, while descriptions such as "near Magenta", "Lombardy", "between Milan and Varese" and "Vergiate" gradually lost their original distinctions as they were repeated.

Some secondary accounts make the geographical confusion particularly visible by incorrectly treating the Vergiate aviation facility as though it were itself at Magenta.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ufoinsight.com/conspiracy/government/the-1933-lombardy-ufo-crash" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufoinsight.com">[UFO Insight]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufoinsight.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the 1933 lombardy ufo crash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">the 1933 lombardy ufo crash</span></span></span> That is not a minor detail: once the alleged recovery area and alleged storage facility are geographically collapsed, a reader can easily infer that Vergiate was the crash location.

## What can actually be said about Vergiate

The strongest historically defensible conclusion is narrower than either the believer or debunker version of the story.

**Vergiate's aviation connection is factual.** SIAI was a major Italian aircraft manufacturer, and the Vergiate plant became an important production and flight-test site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

**Its role in the UFO case is alleged.** Pinotti's reconstruction identified the SIAI aviation complex around Vergiate and Sesto Calende as the probable hiding place for whatever was supposedly recovered. His own explanation shows that the location was inferred from circumstantial considerations rather than established by a securely sourced contemporary storage record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.net/fasfile/gabinetto%20rs33.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.net">[centroufologiconazionale.net]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">UNOPENE D FILE</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNOPENE D FILE</span></span></span>

**The precise Vergiate-hangar claim has a dating problem.** Independent histories place construction of SIAI's Vergiate factory in 1937, whereas the alleged recovery is dated to 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span> An earlier SIAI connection could therefore point instead towards the company's pre-existing operations around Sesto Calende, but that possibility should not be promoted into fact without documentary evidence.

**Nothing found in the stronger historical sources independently verifies UFO wreckage at the plant.** The wider case still depends heavily on anonymously sourced [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) and later interpretations whose archival standing has been challenged. CISU's detailed criticism stresses weaknesses in the evidential value assigned to the supposed Fascist files, while even a review sympathetic to investigating the Magenta case acknowledges the absence of corroborating government documentation for the alleged [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) organisation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theufochronicles.com/2023/08/the-alleged-1933-italian-ufo-crash.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theufochronicles.com">[The UFO Chronicles]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theufochronicles.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">the alleged 1933 italian ufo crash</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The UFO ChroniclesThe Alleged 1933 Italian UFO Crash Under Scrutiny – Fascist UFO FilesAugust 31, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 31, 2023</span></span></span>

For the Magenta-versus-Vergiate question, this produces a useful hierarchy of claims. Calling Vergiate **the 1933 crash site** goes beyond the standard reconstruction. Calling it **the alleged storage site** reflects that reconstruction more accurately. Calling the 1937 Vergiate factory a **verified storage site for an object recovered in 1933** goes further than the surviving public evidence permits.

## The clearest reading of the geography

Vergiate should therefore remain on the **post-recovery side of the story**. The traditional narrative does not require Magenta and Vergiate to compete for the same role: an object could, in principle, be recovered near one place and transported to another. What prevents that sequence from becoming established history is not the distance between the locations but the absence of independently authenticated evidence documenting the recovery and transfer.

More importantly, checking the real industrial chronology exposes a weakness that simplified retellings conceal. SIAI certainly existed in 1933; its long-established centre at Sesto Calende makes an association with the company historically possible. But the famous Vergiate works that later became synonymous with SIAI aviation date from 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.culturadimpresa.org/zabban/portfolio/siai-marchetti-spa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: culturadimpresa.org">[Centro per la cultura d&#x27;impresa]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">culturadimpresa.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on culturadimpresa.org.</span></span></span>

The careful formulation is consequently that **Magenta is the conventional alleged recovery area, while Vergiate is a later proposed place of concealment within the reconstruction developed around the case**. The genuine aerospace history of Vergiate explains why that proposal has endured. It does not transform Vergiate into a documented 1933 crash site—and the 1937 date of the factory means that the most literal version of the familiar "stored in the Vergiate hangars from 1933" claim requires considerably more explanation and evidence than many retellings acknowledge.
