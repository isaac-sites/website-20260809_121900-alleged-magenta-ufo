## Introduction

The date **13 June 1933** has become the standard date of the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) incident for one principal reason: it is the date carried by the most important papers in the first batch of the supposed Fascist-era [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}) that surfaced anonymously in the 1990s. Modern summaries of the case describe that batch as containing two items on Italian Senate stationery and three highly confidential telegrams associated with Milan, all dated 13 June 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

That makes 13 June the **strongest documentary date within the Magenta story**, but not a verified historical crash date. The distinction is essential. The papers have no demonstrated archival [provenance]({{ 'provenance/' | relative_url }}), independent researchers have questioned whether they can properly be treated as official records, and the reported technical examinations have not produced publicly available evidence establishing their authenticity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? No, thank you!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files? No, thank you! - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciAugust 2, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2, 2023</span></span></span>

The most defensible conclusion is therefore narrower than the familiar claim that a UFO crashed at Magenta on 13 June 1933. What can be said is that **13 June is unusually prominent in the documentary tradition from which the modern story developed**. Whether the documents are authentic, whether they refer to a real unusual aircraft, and whether the event they supposedly concern actually happened that same day are separate questions.

## What the 13 June documents actually say

The date's importance begins with the material received by Italian UFO researchers Roberto [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }}) and Alfredo Lissoni. According to later inventories, the first batch arrived in January 1996 and comprised **five alleged 1933 documents**: two papers bearing *Senato del Regno* stationery and three highly confidential telegrams associated with Milan's telegraphic office. All five were dated **13 June 1933**. Later batches dealt with other dates and alleged incidents, but this first group became the foundation for dating the Magenta episode.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

Pinotti's own English-language presentation of the material makes the link explicit. He described telegrams concerning the recovery of the supposed object and a protocol intended to suppress publication, and identified the alleged recovery as taking place on **13 June 1933**. In that telling, the documents concern censorship as well as the recovery itself, which helps explain why one date became attached to both the supposed incident and the government's alleged reaction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span>

At least one image purporting to be one of the [Mussolini]({{ 'mussolini/' | relative_url }}) telegrams has circulated publicly. The scan catalogued on Wikimedia Commons is identified as a telegram supposedly signed on 13 June 1933 and ordering silence concerning a UFO said to have landed [near Magenta]({{ 'near-magenta/' | relative_url }}). The existence of the scan is useful evidence for the **history of the claim**: it shows precisely the sort of dated object on which the June chronology rests. It is not evidence that an Italian government archive has authenticated the telegram.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATelegramma_Mussolini_13_giugno_1933.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJune 13, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 1933</span></span></span>

This distinction becomes particularly important because later descriptions sometimes strengthen the wording. Secondary UFO literature has described the telegrams as orders to recover a flying saucer that had “landed on June 13”, thereby treating the document date and the alleged event date as identical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalufocenter.com/2016/04/filers-files-15-2016-europes-ufo-crashes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalufocenter.com">[nationalufocenter.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalufocenter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">filers files 15 2016 europes ufo crashes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Filer’s Files 15 -2016 Europe’s UFO Crashes - National UFO CenterApril 8, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2016</span></span></span> The surviving public presentation of the evidence does not justify treating that inference as an independently confirmed historical fact.

## Why a document date is not automatically a crash date

Even if every disputed paper were assumed authentic for the sake of argument, **13 June on a telegram would first establish when the telegram was dated**. It would not necessarily establish when the event prompting it occurred.

Government communications can be issued hours or days after the event they concern. A censorship instruction dated 13 June could therefore refer to something that happened earlier. Conversely, a document could describe an event believed by its author to be occurring that day without proving that the author's information was correct. Establishing an incident date normally requires comparison with independent contemporary material: police reports, military logs, local administrative records, diaries, [newspapers]({{ 'newspapers/' | relative_url }}), meteorological or astronomical observations, or documents with a traceable archival history.

That supporting chain has not been demonstrated for the Magenta claim. The critical analysis published by the Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici (CISU) stresses that the supposed Fascist papers arrived from an **anonymous source** and could not be traced to identifiable archival holdings. Giuseppe Stilo consequently argued that they could not simply be treated as documents in the normal historical sense, because researchers could not follow them backwards through an archive to the offices said to have produced them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? Archivist Massimiliano Grandi made the same problem more specific. His assessment, drawing on discussion with former Italian National Archives superintendent and archivistics professor Paola Carucci, emphasised that the unknown origin of the papers and the difficulty independent researchers had in examining them deprived them of the provenance needed for strong historical evidential value. An envelope carrying *Senato del Regno* markings did not solve that problem: period stationery and envelopes can survive independently of whatever documents are later placed inside them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

The result is a useful hierarchy of claims. **“A circulated document is dated 13 June 1933” is directly observable from the material made public. “That document was genuinely produced by Mussolini's administration on 13 June 1933” requires authentication. “A strange aircraft was recovered that day” requires another evidential step again. “The aircraft was extraterrestrial” would require considerably more evidence still.** Retellings of Magenta frequently collapse those separate propositions into a single sentence.

## The authentication problem behind the date

Supporters of the documents have cited examinations of their physical characteristics as evidence that the papers belong to the Fascist period. A recent sympathetic review of the case says forensic testing was reported to have found paper and ink compatible with the 1930s, but also acknowledges the decisive limitation: **full laboratory reports have not been publicly released and the results have not been independently verified**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

More importantly for the 13 June question, dating a sheet of paper is not equivalent to authenticating the information written on it. Grandi's archival critique explicitly distinguishes establishing that material is old from demonstrating that a paper is an authentic official record belonging to a particular archive. He also questioned whether the reported examinations could date the material as precisely as proponents implied.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

That matters because genuine period materials do not automatically authenticate their contents. Old stationery can acquire later writing; authentic blank government envelopes can survive for decades; typography, seals and administrative language can be imitated. Provenance — a demonstrable history connecting a record to the institution that created and preserved it — is therefore especially important where the contents make an extraordinary historical claim.

For the Magenta papers, that chain begins not in a Fascist ministry's surviving archive but with anonymous deliveries more than sixty years later. Pinotti and Lissoni's material reportedly arrived between 1996 and 2000 from a sender identified as “Mr X”, with a mixture of purported originals and photocopies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span> CISU's criticism does not establish that every sheet must therefore be forged, and Stilo explicitly avoided making so categorical a claim. It does establish why **13 June cannot acquire the status of verified history merely because several papers in the same anonymously sourced collection carry that date**.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? ## The 15 June newspaper clue does not independently confirm 13 June

One of the more interesting attempts to give the June chronology external support involved an actual contemporary newspaper. Lissoni drew attention to a *Corriere della Sera* report of **15 June 1933** concerning movements of Italian prefects and interpreted the administrative changes as potentially connected with official agitation following the alleged UFO event. If that interpretation were sustainable, it would be valuable because it would place an independently published contemporary event close to the disputed 13 June date.

The problem is that the connection is interpretative rather than documentary. According to Grandi's account, Paola Carucci rejected the supposed significance of the prefectural reshuffle: movements of prefects were a normal feature of Italian Interior Ministry administration, intended in part to prevent officials remaining in the same locality for excessively long periods. The 15 June report therefore does not independently identify a crash, an unknown aircraft or an emergency response.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-a-professional-archivists-opinion/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files: a Professional Archivist&#x27;s Opinion - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici...</span></span></span>

This example shows why proximity in time is not enough. A genuine newspaper item published two days after the alleged incident can support the Magenta chronology only if there is evidence connecting its subject to the alleged incident. Without that connection, using an ordinary administrative development to corroborate the telegrams risks reasoning backwards from the date that researchers are already trying to prove.

## How later accounts elevated 13 June

Once Pinotti and Lissoni publicised the papers, **13 June migrated from being a date printed on disputed documents into being the date of the alleged crash itself**. Pinotti's published presentation states that the second Italian UFO case was the one in which a landed object was recovered on 13 June 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.theblackvault.com/casefiles/the-ufo-files-of-mussolini-fascist-ufo-files-by-roberto-pinotti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theblackvault.com">[theblackvault.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theblackvault.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">TH E UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THE UFO FILES OF MUSSOLINI: Fascist UFO Files by Roberto Pinotti - The Black Vault Case FilesAugust 10, 2020...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 10, 2020</span></span></span> Later UFO publications repeated the formulation, describing telegrams as instructions relating to a saucer that had landed that day.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://nationalufocenter.com/2016/04/filers-files-15-2016-europes-ufo-crashes/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nationalufocenter.com">[nationalufocenter.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nationalufocenter.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">filers files 15 2016 europes ufo crashes</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Filer’s Files 15 -2016 Europe’s UFO Crashes - National UFO CenterApril 8, 2016...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: April 8, 2016</span></span></span>

The process is visible in contemporary coverage as well. A 2025 Italian account says the alleged Mussolini telegram was dated 13 June and ordered silence about the incident, while a 2026 *Corriere della Sera* retrospective again identifies Pinotti and Lissoni's Fascist-era papers as the basis through which the case entered public discussion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.quotidiano.net/magazine/ufo-mussolini-et3wfqoa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: quotidiano.net">[Quotidiano Nazionale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">quotidiano.net</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Nazionale Il disco segreto di Magenta e gli &#x27;X-Files&#x27; di Mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Quotidiano NazionaleIl disco segreto di Magenta e gli &#x27;X-Files&#x27; di MussoliniOctober 24, 2025 — 24 Oct 2025 — Secondo queste carte, Benito...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 24, 2025</span></span></span> The date has consequently become part of the case's shorthand: “Magenta, 13 June 1933” now functions almost as a name for the event.

There is an additional warning sign within the wider literature: **not every account associated with the Magenta story has used 13 June**. ## Why 13 June remains the strongest date claim

Despite these problems, 13 June still deserves a different evidential status from dates that appear only in later narrative retellings. It is not simply a date chosen retrospectively because it fits the story. It appears repeatedly in the alleged documentary nucleus from which the modern Magenta case emerged. The first batch is reported to contain **five items sharing that date**, and a scan of one alleged telegram carrying 13 June has been publicly circulated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File%3ATelegramma_Mussolini_13_giugno_1933.jpg" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: commons.wikimedia.org">[Wikimedia Commons]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">commons.wikimedia.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">File:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Wikimedia CommonsFile:Telegramma Mussolini 13 giugno 1933.jpg - Wikimedia CommonsJune 13, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 1933</span></span></span>

That makes the most careful assessment a two-part one.

**Within the Magenta source tradition, 13 June 1933 is the strongest date.** It has a clearer documentary anchor than competing dates because multiple papers in the earliest disclosed batch carry it, proponents explicitly linked those papers with the supposed recovery, and later accounts largely inherited that chronology.

**As a matter of independently verified history, 13 June 1933 remains unestablished.** The documents arrived anonymously more than six decades after the supposed event; their archival provenance has not been demonstrated; reported physical examinations do not substitute for authentication; and the nearby contemporary evidence cited to reinforce the chronology has not independently established a UFO crash or recovery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cisu.org/fascist-ufo-files-no-thank-you/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cisu.org">[CISU - Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cisu.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Centro Italiano Studi Ufologici Fascist UFO Files? No, thank you!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciFascist UFO Files? No, thank you! - CISU - Centro Italiano Studi UfologiciAugust 2, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 2, 2023</span></span></span>

The difference between those statements is the key to understanding the date dispute. Calling 13 June the **best-attested date in the alleged RS/33 papers** accurately reflects the surviving claim. Calling it the **confirmed date of the Magenta crash** goes beyond what the available evidence can presently support.
