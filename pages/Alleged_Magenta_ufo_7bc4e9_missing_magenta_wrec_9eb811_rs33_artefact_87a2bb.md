## Introduction

The alleged RS/33 investigation is the part of the Magenta UFO story where claims move from a reported recovery to a supposed government research programme. According to modern accounts, the Fascist Italian government created a secret body called Gabinetto RS/33 after an alleged 1933 incident [near Magenta]({{ 'near-magenta/' | relative_url }}), assigning scientists to examine an unusual “non-conventional vehicle”. However, the central problem remains unchanged: no authenticated artefact attributed to RS/33 has ever been publicly produced for independent laboratory analysis. The surviving case rests on disputed [documents]({{ 'documents/' | relative_url }}), later testimony and claims about lost or transferred material rather than a specimen that researchers can test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because an investigation programme, even if historically real, would not by itself prove the existence or origin of the object it allegedly studied. The unresolved question is narrower and more practical: did [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) leave behind a verifiable [physical evidence]({{ 'physical-evidence/' | relative_url }}) trail, or only a documentary narrative about one?

## The alleged research programme

### What RS/33 is claimed to have been

The name “Gabinetto RS/33” appears in modern retellings of the Magenta case as a supposed special research group established in 1933 to study the recovered object. Supporters of the claim associate the initials with “Ricerche Speciali” (“Special Research”) and connect the group with prominent Italian scientific figures, especially Guglielmo Marconi. Accounts promoted by Italian UFO researchers describe RS/33 as a classified effort involving scientists, military officials and state authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.centroufologiconazionale.eu/rivelazioni-di-david-grusch-sui-file-fascisti-comunicato-stampa-cun/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: centroufologiconazionale.eu">[CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALE]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">centroufologiconazionale.eu</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALERivelazioni di David Grusch sui file fascisti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Comunicato Stampa CUN - CENTRO UFOLOGICO NAZIONALEJune 13, 2023...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 13, 2023</span></span></span>

The documentary basis for these claims emerged decades later. Researcher Roberto [Pinotti]({{ 'pinotti/' | relative_url }}) reported receiving anonymous packets of documents in the 1990s that allegedly related to the Magenta event, including references to RS/33, government secrecy measures and investigations of unidentified aircraft. These materials became the foundation for the modern RS/33 narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that the existence of a document collection is not the same as proof of the historical events described within it. Historians and sceptical investigators have questioned the provenance of the papers, noting that their chain of custody begins with late twentieth-century [anonymous sources]({{ 'anonymous-source/' | relative_url }}) rather than with a documented archival trail from the 1930s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cicap.org/articolo/documenti-desecretati-cosa-ci-aiutano-a-capire-del-fenomeno-ufo" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cicap.org">[CICAP]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cicap.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Documenti desecretati: cosa ci aiutano a capire del fenomeno UFO? &#124; CICAP</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Documenti desecretati: cosa ci aiutano a capire del fenomeno UFO? &#124; CICAP...</span></span></span>

### The evidence problem inside the RS/33 claim

A research programme of the importance claimed for RS/33 would normally be expected to generate multiple independent traces: official correspondence, budgets, personnel records, laboratory notes, storage records, technical reports or surviving institutional references. The publicly available Magenta material has not produced that kind of independently verified archive.

Instead, the strongest RS/33 claims depend on a small group of disputed papers. These reportedly include telegrams, memoranda and references to secret studies, but their interpretation remains contested. Some researchers argue that the documents represent evidence of an extraordinary investigation; critics argue that the papers have not been sufficiently authenticated or independently linked to an actual recovered object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[New Paradigm Institute]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteCase Study Magenta Italy 1933 – New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

The result is an unusual evidentiary situation: the alleged investigation is easier to discuss than the alleged thing being investigated. RS/33 has a narrative identity, but its supposed laboratory subject has no publicly accessible physical identity.

## Claims of stored material

### The missing object at the centre of the story

The RS/33 story usually includes the claim that the recovered object was stored at industrial facilities connected with Italian aviation, particularly SIAI-Marchetti at Vergiate. According to the narrative, authorities secured the object, transported it away from the crash location and kept it under controlled access while scientists examined it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.luxaliena.it/dossier/ufo-di-mussolini/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: luxaliena.it">[Lux Aliena Lab]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">luxaliena.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ufo di mussolini</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Lux Aliena LabL&#x27;UFO di Mussolini (Magenta 1933): fatti, documenti, testimonianze &#124; Lux Aliena...</span></span></span>

If such storage occurred, the most important historical question would be the chain of custody: who handled the object, where was it moved, who authorised transfers, and what happened to it afterwards? Those records have not been publicly demonstrated. The absence of a documented storage history means researchers cannot establish whether an object existed beyond the claims made about it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">magenta incident evidence for ufos 3328fa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is the 1933 Magenta Incident Good Evidence for UFOs?June 15, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2026</span></span></span>

Some versions of the story extend the alleged chain further, claiming the material was later transferred during the Second World War and eventually reached Allied hands. ### Why a storage claim is not the same as physical evidence

A claimed storage location does not substitute for a specimen. A laboratory investigation requires access to the material itself, not only descriptions of where it was supposedly kept.

For a genuine historical artefact, researchers would expect to examine questions such as:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* What materials is it made from?
* Are manufacturing methods consistent with known technology from the period?
* Does the object show signs of aviation, experimental engineering or something unexplained?
* Can independent laboratories reproduce the findings?

</div>

No publicly available RS/33 artefact has reached that stage. As a result, claims about unusual alloys, advanced technology or non-human manufacture remain unsupported by direct [material testing]({{ 'material-tests-b6110a/' | relative_url }}).<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">magenta incident evidence for ufos 3328fa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is the 1933 Magenta Incident Good Evidence for UFOs?June 15, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2026</span></span></span>

## The missing laboratory specimen

### Why the absence changes the debate

The missing specimen is the central weakness of the RS/33 claim. Documents can show that people wrote about an object, believed they had found something unusual, or organised an inquiry. They cannot reveal the physical properties of an object that is unavailable for examination.

This is why the lack of authenticated wreckage is more significant than the lack of a single missing report. A lost report could eventually be replaced by other records. A missing physical sample removes the possibility of direct testing altogether.

The Magenta case therefore remains at the level of historical allegation rather than laboratory-confirmed discovery. The available record supports the existence of a modern controversy about RS/33, but it does not provide an independently verified artefact from that alleged investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://factually.co/fact-checks/science/magenta-incident-evidence-for-ufos-3328fa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: factually.co">[Factually]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">factually.co</span><span class="citation-popover-title">magenta incident evidence for ufos 3328fa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Is the 1933 Magenta Incident Good Evidence for UFOs?June 15, 2026...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 15, 2026</span></span></span>
