## Introduction

The safest answer is that **“Magenta” is the conventional name of the alleged 1933 UFO case, while Vergiate is better documented within the story as the supposed destination of the recovered object rather than as an independently established crash site**. Modern retellings sometimes blur those two roles, describing the event as happening “between Magenta and Vergiate” or even placing the crash at Vergiate itself. The surviving public evidence does not justify that precision.

## Why the location remains uncertain

The largest problem is not choosing between Magenta and Vergiate. It is that the public evidence does not securely establish a crash at either.

The modern case depends heavily on papers that Pinotti and collaborators said began arriving anonymously in 1996. According to the New Paradigm Institute's review, the first batch consisted of letters and purported telegrams dated 13 June 1933; later material included a handwritten memorandum, a drawing and references to the alleged [RS/33]({{ 'rs-33/' | relative_url }}) organisation. The same review notes that claims of forensic testing on the paper and ink have not been supported by publicly released full laboratory reports permitting independent verification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://newparadigminstitute.org/learn/library/case-study-magenta-italy-1933/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: newparadigminstitute.org">[newparadigminstitute.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">newparadigminstitute.org</span><span class="citation-popover-title">case study magenta italy 1933</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">New Paradigm InstituteAugust 11, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 11, 2025</span></span></span>

## Magenta or Vergiate?

If the case needs a concise geographical description, **“the alleged 1933 recovery near Magenta, with the object supposedly transferred to aviation facilities at Vergiate”** most accurately preserves the distinction found in the better-developed versions of the story.

Calling it simply the **Magenta UFO crash** is reasonable as a conventional case name, especially because that is how Grusch and much of the later international discussion identify it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.congress.gov/118/meeting/house/116282/documents/HHRG-118-GO06-20230726-SD006.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: congress.gov">[Congress.gov]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">congress.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">We are not alone: The UFO whistleblower speaks</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">November 29, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: November 29, 2025</span></span></span> Calling Vergiate the confirmed crash site goes further than the evidence allows. Vergiate's historically real aerospace importance explains why it features so prominently, but it does not independently verify either the presence of an anomalous wreck there or the location where such an object supposedly came down.

The unresolved geography is therefore not a minor cartographic detail. It exposes one of the central evidential limitations of the entire location claim: after decades of retelling, researchers can identify a **broad Lombardy corridor and several recurring place names**, but not an independently documented patch of ground that can confidently be called the 1933 crash site.
