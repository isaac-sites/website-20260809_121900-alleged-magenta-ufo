## Introduction

The available historical record does **not** support the often-repeated claim that a SIAI aircraft factory with the later, familiar Vergiate hangars was already operating there in 1933. The strongest evidence points the other way: in 1933 SIAI's documented works were at **[Sesto Calende]({{ 'sesto-calende/' | relative_url }})**, while the land used for the Vergiate expansion was acquired only in the mid-1930s and the new factory and airfield were developed from about **1937**. A contemporary 1933 reference in the *Enciclopedia Italiana* explicitly located SIAI's workshops at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

That chronology creates a significant problem for versions of the [alleged Magenta UFO]({{ 'alleged-magenta-ufo/' | relative_url }}) story which say the recovered object was immediately hidden in "the SIAI-Marchetti hangars at Vergiate" in 1933. It does not prove that nothing could possibly have been stored somewhere in the Vergiate area. It does mean that the specific hangar claim requires [independent evidence]({{ 'independent-evidence/' | relative_url }}) rather than being accepted simply because Vergiate later became one of Italy's important aircraft-production centres.

## What company histories say about SIAI's early bases

SIAI's origins are firmly associated with Sesto Calende, several kilometres north of Vergiate. The company was founded there in 1915 as the Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia. Museo Agusta's company history says its seaplane base was established at Sant'Anna on Lake Maggiore, where flying instruction and aircraft construction began; Alessandro Marchetti became technical director in 1922.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span> Treccani likewise identifies Sesto Calende as the company's founding location.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span>

More importantly, there is unusually useful evidence from **the year in question itself**. The 1933 *Enciclopedia Italiana*, in its survey of the Italian aircraft industry, listed the "Società idrovolanti Alta Italia (SIAI)" as having its workshops at **Sesto Calende**. It listed other manufacturers and their locations in the same passage, making this a straightforward industrial-geographical description rather than a retrospective company legend. Vergiate is not identified as a SIAI manufacturing site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - Enciclopedia - TreccaniJanuary 1, 1933...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: January 1, 1933</span></span></span>

An official Italian government document from September 1933 supplies a second contemporary anchor. The *Gazzetta Ufficiale del Regno d'Italia* described Alessandro Marchetti as technical director of the Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia **"in Sesto Calende"**. That does not catalogue every property the company might conceivably have owned or rented, but it independently confirms where SIAI was institutionally identified in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gazzettaufficiale.it/eli/gu/1933/09/30/228/sg/pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gazzettaufficiale.it">[Gazzetta Ufficiale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gazzettaufficiale.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Gazzetta Ufficiale GAZZETTA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UF I PARTE PRIMA DEL REGNO D&#x27;ITALIA Si...</span></span></span>

Local historical material agrees. The Comune di Sesto Calende describes the Sant'Anna seaplane base and notes that the SIAI aircraft used in the celebrated 1933 Atlantic flight were built at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sesto Calende Comune di Sesto Calende</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Parco EuropaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span> The Provincia di Varese likewise identifies Sant'Anna as the seaplane centre used during the 1920s and 1930s.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://oldsite.provincia.va.it/code/40914/Ex-Idroscalo-Sant-Anna-Parco-Europa" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: oldsite.provincia.va.it">[oldsite.provincia.va.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">oldsite.provincia.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Ex Idroscalo Sant&#x27;Anna (Parco Europa</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sesto Calende...</span></span></span>

Taken together, these sources make Sesto Calende the securely documented SIAI centre in 1933. That is a materially different proposition from saying that SIAI had the later Vergiate aircraft works operating in that year.

## Why the 1937 production date matters

The most important evidence comes from Vergiate's own historical chronology. ## Could there still have been some facility at Vergiate in 1933?

This is where the evidence needs careful wording. The historical material establishes a chronology problem for the **claimed SIAI hangars**; it does not establish the logically broader proposition that Vergiate contained no shed, warehouse, industrial building or usable piece of property whatsoever in 1933.

The site had an industrial history before SIAI arrived. Vergiate had contained the Rossi explosives works, which suffered a devastating explosion in 1920. The municipal historical account specifically says Savoia-Marchetti acquired land occupied by that former facility in 1935.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://storage.googleapis.com/stu-vergiate-public-prod/2023-05/RA%202%20-%20Quadro%20conoscitivo%20-%20I%20Parte_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: storage.googleapis.com">[Google Cloud Storage]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">storage.googleapis.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Google Cloud Storage VAS – Rapporto Ambientale Comune di Vergiate</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Google Cloud StorageVAS – Rapporto Ambientale Comune di VergiateJune 7, 2014...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 7, 2014</span></span></span> A separate treatment of the same local history also places the Savoia-Marchetti acquisition after the explosives works and says an airfield and other industrial buildings followed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.andinrete.it/portale/documenti/pdf/Mappando%20il%20tesoro%20azzardato%20WEB.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: andinrete.it">[andinrete.it]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">andinrete.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mappando il tesoro azzardato. Esiti di una mappatura Geo</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mappando il tesoro azzardato. Esiti di una mappatura Geo</span></span></span>

That distinction matters. A claim that an object was temporarily placed in **some pre-existing building near Vergiate** would require one kind of evidence. A claim that it was taken to **SIAI-Marchetti's Vergiate hangars** implies an established company installation that the presently available industrial histories date several years later.

Nor should later terminology be projected backwards casually. Treccani notes that the company was founded as Società Idrovolanti Alta Italia and underwent several name changes, with "Marchetti" formally entering the company name in 1937.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Siai-marchetti - Enciclopedia - Treccani...</span></span></span> Calling a hypothetical 1933 building a "SIAI-Marchetti hangar at Vergiate" therefore combines both a location strongly associated with the firm's later expansion and a corporate name formalised later. Neither point alone disproves the underlying UFO story, but together they warn that modern retellings may be describing 1933 through the geography and terminology of later decades.

## The UFO narrative does not resolve the contradiction

Modern accounts of the alleged incident frequently state that wreckage was taken to SIAI facilities at Vergiate. A 2022 local article, for example, describes the remains as being taken to SIAI-Marchetti facilities at Vergiate and Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.varesenoi.it/2022/06/18/sommario/la-varese-nascosta/leggi-notizia/argomenti/la-varese-nascosta/articolo/la-varese-nascosta-quellufo-precipitato-sul-varesotto-novantanni-dopo-e-ancora-mistero.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: varesenoi.it">[Varese Noi]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">varesenoi.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Varese Noi LA VARESE NASCOSTA. Quell&#x27;Ufo precipitato sul Varesotto</span></span></span> Other retellings simplify this further into storage in "the SIAI-Marchetti hangars at Vergiate".

The difficulty is that repetition is not independent confirmation. These later accounts belong to the modern UFO narrative, whereas the industrial chronology can be checked against contemporary reference material, municipal histories and aviation-heritage records. When those bodies of evidence conflict, the dated industrial sources deserve greater weight for the narrow factual question of **what SIAI infrastructure existed where in 1933**.

The possibility of confusion with Sesto Calende is particularly important. In the very year of the alleged incident, Sesto Calende genuinely was producing historically important SIAI aircraft. The town's official history notes that the S.55X aircraft associated with Italo Balbo's 1933 Atlantic crossing were manufactured there.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.comune.sesto-calende.va.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/Parco-Europa/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: comune.sesto-calende.va.it">[Sesto Calende]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">comune.sesto-calende.va.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Sesto Calende Comune di Sesto Calende</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sesto CalendeComune di Sesto Calende - Parco EuropaJuly 23, 2025...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 23, 2025</span></span></span> Museo Agusta likewise places the company's original production and seaplane operations at Sesto Calende.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.museoagusta.it/la-siai-marchetti/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: museoagusta.it">[Museo Agusta]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">museoagusta.it</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Museo Agusta La SIAI Marchetti</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Museo AgustaLa SIAI Marchetti - Museo Agusta...</span></span></span>

That is only a plausible explanation, not proof of how the discrepancy arose. But it demonstrates why the place-name cannot be treated as a trivial detail.

## What would verify a 1933 Vergiate facility?

To overturn or substantially qualify the current chronology, evidence would need to show more than that SIAI later operated at Vergiate. Ideally it would establish a company-controlled building there **before the alleged recovery** in 1933.

The most useful evidence would be dated land-registry records, property deeds, building permits, factory plans, company ledgers, insurance records, aerial [photographs]({{ 'photographs/' | relative_url }}) or SIAI correspondence identifying a Vergiate property in 1933. Contemporary maps showing a hangar or aviation installation could also be important, particularly if ownership could be established. Fondazione Leonardo's historical SIAI-Marchetti archive is potentially relevant because its holdings preserve photographs, technical drawings and company papers from the firm's history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://lia.fondazioneleonardo.com/archivi/s/lia/item-set/20067?page=2&amp;sort_order=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lia.fondazioneleonardo.com">[lia.fondazioneleonardo.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lia.fondazioneleonardo.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">item set</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">item set</span></span></span>

There would also need to be precision about **which building** is being claimed. Evidence that an old explosives-factory structure survived at Vergiate in 1933 would not establish that it was a SIAI hangar. Evidence that SIAI acquired the property in 1935 would not prove possession two years earlier. And photographs of the famous later Vergiate aircraft works cannot demonstrate their existence in 1933 unless the photographs themselves are securely dated to that period.

Conversely, proving that the later SIAI factory did not yet exist would not eliminate every imaginable storage scenario. A revised claim might place the alleged material at Sesto Calende, in a military building, or in some unrelated industrial structure. But each such revision would require its own evidence. It cannot simply inherit credibility from the later existence of the Vergiate aircraft works.

## The chronology weighs against the claimed hangars

For the narrow question — **were the claimed SIAI hangars at Vergiate there in 1933?** — the best-supported answer is **apparently not, at least not the documented SIAI aircraft-production complex described in later accounts**.

The strongest contemporary evidence identifies SIAI's workshops with Sesto Calende in 1933.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.treccani.it/enciclopedia/italia_%28Enciclopedia-Italiana%29/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: treccani.it">[Treccani]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">treccani.it</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Italia - That makes the hangar story a genuine chronological weakness in attempts to identify Vergiate as the immediate 1933 storage location. It is not conclusive evidence against every version of the alleged Magenta event, because an unidentified earlier building or a different destination remains logically possible. But until a dated record demonstrates SIAI-controlled premises at Vergiate in 1933, describing the alleged wreckage as having been hidden in the established "SIAI-Marchetti hangars at Vergiate" projects a **later aviation complex backwards into an earlier year**.
